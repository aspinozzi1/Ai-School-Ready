Is this goal on pace? The zero-technology check

No chart, no AI, no login — just read the last three data points against the target and match your situation below.

What you're seeingWhat it usually meansA reasonable next step
Last three scores are flat or falling, well below targetThe current approach may not be reaching this studentBring the data to the team; consider a different teaching strategy or more frequent practice
Scores are rising, but slowlyLikely on pace, just earlyKeep the current plan; re-check in two more weeks
Scores are rising quickly, near or at criterionThe goal may be close to metConfirm with one more data point at criterion, then flag for the team to consider next steps
Scores bounce up and down with no clear directionThe measurement or the conditions may be inconsistentCheck that trials are given the same way each time before changing the goal itself

The AI Progress Summary page

This is the page nothing else in this niche has: turn six weeks of de-identified scores into a trend read and a drafted progress-report sentence. Use a student number only. Never paste a name, a class roster, or a diagnosis.

Below pace On pace, early Meeting or near criterion
Worked example — Student #14, two-digit addition with regrouping, criterion 8/10 correct on 2 consecutive weekly checks. Six weekly scores, out of 10: 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8.

Trend: steady increase, +4 points over six weeks, no week scored lower than the one before it.
Read against the criterion: this week's score of 8/10 meets the criterion for the first time. One more consecutive check at 8/10 or higher would meet the full two-check criterion.
What this is not: not a claim that the goal is met, and not a recommendation about whether to write a new goal — both stay the team's call once the second check comes in.

Copy-paste prompt: trend read + a drafted sentence

"Here are a student's weekly progress-monitoring scores, most recent last, out of a possible total: [PASTE SCORES ONLY — NO NAME, NO CLASS ROSTER, NO DISABILITY LABEL]. The goal's criterion is: [PASTE THE CRITERION FROM THE GOAL-AT-A-GLANCE PAGE]. In three sentences: describe the trend, say whether the scores are moving toward that criterion, and draft one plain-language sentence a case manager could use in a progress report. Do not state whether the goal has been met — only describe the data."

The chatbot drafts a sentence from numbers. Whether the goal is met, whether it changes, and what happens next stay the IEP team's decisions — every time, for every student.

Filing this pack

  • One folder per goal, not per student, if a student has more than one goal you track closely — it keeps the graph and the data sheet for that goal together.
  • Start a fresh weekly data sheet the moment one fills up rather than squeezing extra weeks into the margin.
  • File by student number in a locked cabinet or a password-protected folder, matching your district's storage policy for special education records.
  • Shred or securely delete a goal's working sheets once the official progress report is filed, per your district's retention schedule.

De-identification, always

Before any page in this pack goes into a chatbot
Student number, grade, goal area, and scores only.
Never a name, never a full roster, never a diagnosis or disability label.

This applies to every prompt in this pack, including the AI Progress Summary page. If a sentence you're about to paste would let a stranger identify the student, take out the detail that does it before you paste.

What this pack is not

  • Not an IEP. It tracks progress toward a goal the team already wrote.
  • Not a compliance document. Follow your district's official progress-reporting process and forms; use these pages as your own working data, not the official record unless your district says otherwise.
  • Not legal or clinical advice. It never states whether a student qualifies for services, what a goal should say, or what placement is appropriate.
