30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session built one differentiated artifact per person; the month builds the one-artifact-a-week habit. About 30 minutes of existing PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet: teach with the lab artifact, level and verify one more text, post a prompt to the staff doc. Facilitator reads the exit-ticket "task I still want help with" column. All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Level Check" (10 min, below): the team verifies one AI-leveled text together and calibrates the check-the-level habit. PLC leads
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "Two Directions" (10 min, below): everyone differentiates one live artifact in both directions, support and stretch, on the spot. PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "Artifact Round" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month: prompt-doc growth, artifacts taught, one classroom story. Schedule Kit 4, AI for Assessment. PLC leads · Admin
The quiet goal of the month: one differentiated artifact per teacher per week becomes normal, the staff prompt doc grows a differentiation section, and "check the level" is something colleagues say to each other without prompting.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Level Check"  Week 2 · 10 min

Setup (2 min): one volunteer brings an AI-leveled passage they made this week, easier version on screen or printed.

Check (6 min): the team runs the habit together: one person reads a paragraph aloud; the group eyeballs sentence length; anyone who teaches that grade band gives the verdict. Is it actually at the labeled level? Fix the worst paragraph live with one follow-up prompt ("shorter sentences; keep the key idea").

Close (2 min): agree on the team's default level-check (most teams pick read-aloud plus one-student tryout) and note it in the staff prompt doc.

Follow-up #2 · "Two Directions"  Week 3 · 10 min

Setup (1 min): everyone opens one artifact from a lesson they're teaching this week (a text, practice set, or directions).

Build (7 min): each person runs one support move (level or scaffold) and one stretch move on it, using the handout prompts. Pairs swap screens for a 60-second 4-point review of each other's support version.

Close (2 min): quick round: which direction was harder to get right? Prompts that worked go in the staff doc.

Follow-up #3 · "Artifact Round"  Week 4 · 10 min

Round (6 min): 30 seconds each: the best differentiated artifact you taught with this month, and what the support kids or stretch kids did that they wouldn't have done otherwise. Facilitator collects one story for the board/community update.

Audit (3 min): skim the staff doc's differentiation section: which moves are covered (level, scaffold, stretch, reformat)? Assign one volunteer per gap.

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 4 date.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: ask three teachers, casually, what they built in the lab; artifacts you can name are artifacts that got taught
  • Week 2: respond to the top "differentiation task I still want help with" theme in your staff memo
  • Week 3: check that the differentiation help is reaching every team, not just the early adopters; RAND's finding that higher-poverty schools lag in AI use is a within-building warning too. Pair a confident user with each hesitant team.
  • Week 4: collect one concrete story for the board ("one passage became three levels in ten minutes, and it was verified before students saw it") and schedule Kit 4

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
The habit is realMost staff can show one differentiated artifact from the past week, not just from the session
Verification is normalYou overhear "did you check the level?" in the workroom; labels get tested, not trusted
The doc is growingThe staff prompt doc has a differentiation section with entries from multiple teams
Stretch kids surfaceExtension stories come up unprompted; early finishers are doing depth work, not second worksheets

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Artifacts got built but never taughtShrink the ask: one artifact, one class period, this week. Follow-up #3's story round makes taught artifacts the visible norm.
Leveled texts go out unverifiedRun "Level Check" again with a real example that was off. One caught label teaches more than a policy reminder.
Only the support direction gets usedName it kindly at a staff meeting: extension is differentiation too. Ask two teachers to share a stretch prompt that worked.
Someone pastes student details into a promptCoach privately; re-anchor to the Kit 1 rule at the next meeting without naming names: describe needs, never names. The rule is the floor, always.
