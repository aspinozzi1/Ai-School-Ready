30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session built one reusable prompt per person; the month grows a library. About 30 minutes of existing PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet. Facilitator checks the staff prompt doc, nudges stragglers with one friendly line, and reads the exit-ticket "task I still want help with" column. All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Makeover Minute" (10 min, below): the team rewrites one weak prompt from real work using the four parts. PLC leads
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "Template Swap" (10 min, below): everyone runs a colleague's template on their own content and reports back. PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "Library Audit" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month: prompt-doc entries, time-saved stories. Schedule Kit 3, Planning & Differentiation. PLC leads · Admin
The quiet goal of the month: the staff prompt doc becomes a living tool with entries from most of the staff, at least one shared template per team, and a visible habit of iterating before settling.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Makeover Minute"  Week 2 · 10 min

Setup (2 min): one volunteer shares a prompt from their real work that underdelivered, on screen or read aloud.

Makeover (6 min): the team rebuilds it with the four parts, out loud: what's the role? the task verb? what context would a substitute need? what format? Run the before and after live if a device is handy; the delta sells itself.

Close (2 min): the improved version goes in the staff prompt doc, credited to the whole team.

Follow-up #2 · "Template Swap"  Week 3 · 10 min

Swap (6 min): everyone opens the staff prompt doc, picks a template they didn't write, and runs it on their own content. Elementary tries the CTE safety-quiz template; the math teacher tries the newsletter one.

Report (3 min): quick round: did it travel? What did you change? Edits that make a template more universal go back into the doc.

Close (1 min): tag the two most-borrowed templates as "staff picks."

Follow-up #3 · "Library Audit"  Week 4 · 10 min

Audit (5 min): as a team, skim the prompt doc: what job families are covered (communication, planning, assessment)? What's missing? Assign one volunteer per gap to draft a template before Kit 3.

Stories (4 min): a 30-second round: the best time-save of the month, or the most useful failure (an AI answer somebody caught and fixed). Facilitator collects one story for the board/community update.

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 3 date.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: open the staff prompt doc yourself and add one entry; nothing normalizes a tool like the principal using it
  • Week 2: respond to the top "task I still want help with" theme in your staff memo
  • Week 3: collect one concrete time-saved story for the board; numbers beat adjectives ("the Friday newsletter now takes 10 minutes")
  • Week 4: schedule Kit 3 and note who's emerging as prompt-library champions; they're future facilitators

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
The formula is habitStaff name role · task · context · format unprompted, and prompts in the doc show all four
The library is aliveThe prompt doc has entries you didn't put there, and "staff picks" get borrowed across teams
Iteration is normalYou overhear "push back on it" in the workroom; first drafts get edited, not shipped
Honesty holdsAt least one shared story is about catching AI being wrong

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
The prompt doc is emptySeed it: facilitator posts three templates from the session and tags three likely contributors by name, kindly.
Prompts in the doc are vague one-linersRun Makeover Minute on one of them (with the author's blessing); upgrading a real entry teaches the room more than a fresh example.
One team dominates; others opt outTemplate Swap fixes this: assign cross-team pairs so every department both gives and borrows.
Someone ships an unreviewed AI draft to familiesCoach privately; re-anchor to the Kit 1 review rule at the next meeting without naming names. The rule is the floor, always.
