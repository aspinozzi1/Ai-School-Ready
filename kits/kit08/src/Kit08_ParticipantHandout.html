Building Your School's AI Culture

The stance
Culture is what keeps working when the PD ends.
A tool becomes culture when nobody has to remember to use it.

The culture test

What would a new hire learn by watching, before anyone told them anything? If they'd see prompts shared, drafts reviewed, lanes labeled, and no student names near a chatbot: culture. If they'd have to be told: policy. Policies inform; cultures train.

The sharing routine: win · fail · steal

Five minutes inside a meeting that already exists. One win (an AI-drafted thing that worked) · one fail (what it got wrong, shared without embarrassment: failure shared is quality control) · one steal (a template someone's taking from the library this week).

Skeptic partners (respected, recruited, named)

The colleagues whose doubts keep the culture honest get a real role: reviewers, drift-spotters, the people we ask "what's wrong with this?" first.

Next semester's rhythm (minutes inside existing meetings)

  • Sharing round: every PLC or every other one
  • One 10-minute kit refresher a month (the 30-day plans are the material)
  • Norms revisited in the first month back; new-hire Kit 1 as needed

Measuring without surveillance

Count artifacts, never people: library growth · exit tickets · sharing-round stories · lane labels on assignments. Never audit an individual teacher's AI use; surveillance kills the openness that keeps the culture safe.
