Building Your School's AI Culture

The Track A capstone: your faculty drafts its own five AI norms, calendars a sharing routine, and plans onboarding for every future hire. Run by your own coach; no consultant required.

The stance, and your board answer
Culture is what keeps working when the PD ends. Our staff wrote and signed our AI norms, our routines keep the practice alive, and every new hire is covered.

Why this matters now

Eight weeks of skills currently live in individual teachers, and the research is blunt: PD without sustained routines and collaboration fades (Darling-Hammond et al., 2017). Meanwhile most schools' written guidance lags their staff's actual AI use (RAND, 2025). This session converts training into systems, at a cost of minutes inside meetings that already exist.

What your school has when it's done

  • Five AI norms in the faculty's own words: signed, posted, revisited each semester
  • A five-minute win-fail-steal sharing routine with a date and an owner
  • An onboarding plan: Kit 1 + norms page + library tour for every new hire
  • Skeptic partners named and respected as the culture's quality control
  • A measurement stance: artifacts, never surveillance of individual teachers
  • The AI-Ready Educator Certificate of Completion for every educator finishing Kits 1–8

The three promises leadership makes

The session asks leadership for three public sentences: the five minutes are protected; recovered hours are not refilled with new initiatives; individual AI use is never audited. Small enough to grant on the spot, and together they buy more trust than any tool purchase.

Research base in brief
