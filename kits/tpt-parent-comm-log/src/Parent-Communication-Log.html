The AI safe-prompt companion

Every entry above stays on paper. When you're ready to draft the actual message, describe the pattern from your log — never the child.

Turn a log row into a family email

"Draft a factual, warm-but-direct email to a family about [DESCRIBE THE PATTERN FROM YOUR LOG, e.g. 'three missing homework assignments this week']. Include one positive observation, the facts, and one clear next step. No student name — I'll add that myself before sending."

Summarize a log for a conference

"Turn these contact-log entries into a short conference-ready summary, 3-4 sentences, neutral tone: [PASTE YOUR NOTES WITH NAMES REMOVED]. End with one question I could ask the family."

Filing tips

  • Alphabetical, in a binder, is the fastest system to search under pressure — resist the urge to sort by "type of issue" instead.
  • Start a fresh page the moment one fills up rather than squeezing in tiny handwriting — a log you can't read back isn't documentation.
  • Follow your district's retention policy for how long to keep filled pages, and where signed originals belong versus your working copy.
  • Photograph or scan a backup of any incident log page the same week you write it — paper gets lost exactly when you need it most.
