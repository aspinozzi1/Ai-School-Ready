Build Your First Classroom Tool

A 45–60 minute, self-facilitated staff session. Every teacher leaves with a working file, not a handout: a review game they built from their own questions, on their own device, that runs with the wifi off. Run by your own coach or teacher-leader; no consultant required.

Why this matters now

Maryland's 2026 AI Ready Schools Act sets up statewide AI training for teachers, with the law's own intent of training educators by July 1, 2027, and says teachers must be compensated for it and may not be charged. Virginia and Ohio also passed 2026 laws (Ohio requires a district AI policy by July 1, 2026). FutureEd counted 77 AI-in-education bills in 27 states this session. Meanwhile, Gallup and the Walton Family Foundation found 82% of teachers report receiving no formal guidance on applying AI to their work, and an IBM and Morning Consult survey found 20% of K-12 educators report extensive AI training, with 42% naming a lack of training as the top barrier to AI literacy.

What every teacher can do afterward

  • Run the loop: describe, build, test, change one thing
  • Put their own questions into a working game and save it as their own file
  • Read AI-drafted questions critically and fix what is wrong
  • Recover from the predictable AI mistakes using written fix sentences
  • Keep student data out of every prompt and file
  • Say honestly what they did: changed a working program, did not write one

What is in the kit

Nine files: an editable 34-slide deck with speaker notes, a word-for-word facilitator script, prep guide, participant handout, First 48 Hours sheet, 30-day plan with three 10-minute PLC follow-ups, an exit ticket that doubles as PD documentation, this one-pager, and verified APA references. The lab's starter game file is included. Zero student data anywhere. Full citations for every figure above are in the References file.
