Build Your First Classroom Tool

The loop
Describe · Build · Test · Change one thing
Then test again.

Your lab, in five numbered steps

  1. Open the starter file. Double-click Kit03_ReviewGameStarter.html. It opens in your browser. Play a few questions so you know what you are changing.
  2. Press "Put my own questions in" on the opening screen. This opens the setup screen, one page with everything you need.
  3. Fill in the setup screen. Give the game a title, a place to travel to, and what the round reviews. Then add your questions by any of the three ways below.
  4. Make two changes by describing them, one cosmetic and one behavioral (below). Test after each one.
  5. Press "Save my game as a file." Your browser saves a single file that has your questions already in it. That file is yours to keep, copy and hand out.
No student information, ever. No child's name, score, grade, IEP note, behavior record or writing sample, in any prompt or in your game. The game holds questions, answers and a title.

Three ways to fill in the setup screen

WayHow it works
1 · Type themPress Add a question. Each question card has the question, four answers (mark the right one), an idea (two or three words naming the concept), a level (1 easy, 2 medium, 3 hard), and a why line that teaches after a wrong answer.
2 · Paste a listOne question per line, eight parts separated by the | character:
question | correct answer | wrong | wrong | wrong | idea | level | why
Press Load these questions. The page tells you any line it skipped and why.
3 · Let AI draft a roundType your topic into What this round reviews, press Copy the prompt, paste it into your AI tool, then paste the answer into the box marked Paste what the AI gave you here.
Fast is not the same as right. AI drafts, the teacher decides. Read every drafted question before anyone plays it. Look for a wrong answer that is too easy to rule out, a fact that differs from what you teach, and a why line that does not teach on its own. Fix them in the form.

Use three or four ideas per round

The game tracks mastery by idea. It brings a missed idea back later, and it never asks two questions on the same idea in a row. Give every question on the same concept the same idea words. You need some questions at each level, because students choose their own difficulty by choosing the road.

Your two changes: one cosmetic, one behavioral

Give your AI tool the game file, then describe one change. Always end with the boundary sentence. Save a copy of your file first.

Cosmetic: "Change the color palette to my school colors, [color] and [color]. Keep everything else the same."

Behavioral: "Give the player one more road to choose from at every fork. Keep the rest of the game exactly the way it works now."

More cosmetic ideas: a new title font, a different background color, a bigger question text. More behavioral ideas: show the correct answer for two seconds longer, add a "hint" button, end the game after ten questions.

The boundary sentence ("keep everything else the same") does real work. It is what stops the AI from touching things you never asked it to touch.

Save a copy before every prompt

Before any change you are not sure about, make a second copy of the file. Thirty seconds, no exceptions. The most likely problem in this lab is a change that rewrites more of the file than you expected. A saved copy turns that into a quick reset.

When it breaks

What you seeWhat to do
A blank pageAlmost always a small typo in what the AI just wrote. Say "it is a blank page, nothing shows up," or copy the error message from the browser, paste it back and say "fix this."
It changed the wrong thingClose the file without saving. Reopen your saved copy. Ask again, narrower: "change only the title text, nothing else in the file."
My questions disappearedReopen your saved copy, or press Put my own questions in and paste your questions back in.
The page skipped my linesIt names every skipped line and why. Fix those lines, or paste the message back to the AI and say "fix this."

Track 2: start from nothing, by prompting

For the confident. Paste this as your first prompt into any AI coding tool, then test it, then change one thing at a time. Save a copy after every version that works.

"Build me a browser-based review game in one HTML file, no installs, that works offline. It should track which questions I get wrong and bring them back later in the same game, never let one student's wrong answer end the game for the group, and never show a public list of student names or scores."

Track 2 is a normal path, not a harder one. You will still need your own questions, so keep the setup-screen list of eight parts handy.
