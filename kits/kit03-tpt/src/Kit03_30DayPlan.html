30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session gave every teacher a working game file; the month gets those files moving between classrooms. About 30 minutes of existing PLC or staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet. Facilitator collects three volunteers' game files and reads the exit tickets' "what will you use it for first" answers.All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Bug Swap" (10 min, below).PLC leads
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "One New Change" (10 min, below).PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "Share the File" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month.PLC leads · Admin
The quiet goal of the month: a department where four games exist and get swapped. The files matter more than the stories.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Bug Swap"  Week 2 · 10 min

Setup (2 min): each person names one thing that went wrong when they used their game or the AI: a blank page, a wrong change, a question the AI drafted with a bad fact.

Fix (6 min): in pairs, trade the problem and apply the recovery line (read the error to the AI, or reopen the saved copy and ask again, narrower). Say aloud what the fix sentence was.

Close (2 min): the room names the one fix sentence worth remembering.

Follow-up #2 · "One New Change"  Week 3 · 10 min

Choose (2 min): each person picks one change they wish their game had that the lab did not cover: a hint button, a shorter round, a different look.

Try (6 min): describe it to the AI, with the boundary sentence, after saving a copy. Test it. Keep it or go back to the copy.

Close (2 min): two volunteers say what worked and what they had to narrow.

Follow-up #3 · "Share the File"  Week 4 · 10 min

Swap (6 min): everyone sends their saved game file to one colleague in another grade or subject, and opens the one they receive. Play three questions. Share the file, not the story.

Report (3 min): did it open on the first try? Was anything wrong or unclear in the questions? Note anything to correct.

Close (1 min): the facilitator asks for one file for the shared drive.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: play a colleague's game yourself; nothing normalizes a tool like the principal using it
  • Week 2: read the exit-ticket answers and name the most common first use
  • Week 3: ask two teachers to show a student playing their game, and how the students responded
  • Week 4: count how many game files exist and how many were swapped

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you will know
The loop is habitStaff say "save a copy first" and narrow their requests unprompted
Files are movingGames made by one teacher are being played in another's room
Judgment holdsTeachers read AI-drafted questions before students see them, and can name one they changed
The rule holdsNo game or prompt contains a child's name, score or record

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Nobody played it with studentsMake the swap the ask: play a colleague's file with your own class for ten minutes.
Questions are weakRun Bug Swap on the questions themselves: too easy, too tricky, a wrong fact. The teacher decides.
Files will not open on a school deviceCheck with your technology contact about opening single HTML files in a browser. Do not work around district rules.
Someone puts student data in a promptCoach privately; re-anchor to the Kit 1 rule at the next meeting without naming names. The rule is the floor, always.
