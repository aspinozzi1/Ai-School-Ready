References

APA style. The note beneath each entry says exactly where the kit uses it, so any claim can be traced to its source. Numbers are stated as the source states them: surveys "report," and laws "require" what their text says.

Gallup, & Walton Family Foundation. (2026, May 27). Teacher survey on AI guidance [Survey fielded February 9–March 2, 2026; more than 2,000 public school teachers; reported in N. Modan, Most teachers receive no formal AI guidance for learning and instruction, K-12 Dive]. https://www.k12dive.com/news/most-teachers-receive-no-formal-ai-guidance-for-learning-and-instruction-g/821146/ Used for: 82% of teachers report receiving no formal guidance on applying AI tools to their work across multiple types of work tasks (deck slide 4; script; admin one-pager). Cited as reported in K-12 Dive.

IBM, & Morning Consult. (2026, September 2). New IBM study finds AI adoption is outpacing K-12 readiness [Press release; survey fielded July 2026; 1,019 K-12 education professionals and 1,029 parents]. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-ibm-study-finds-ai-adoption-is-outpacing-k-12-readiness-302866692.html Used for: 20% of K-12 educators report receiving extensive AI training; lack of training or professional development is the top barrier to AI literacy, at 42% (deck slide 4; script; admin one-pager). Cited as a press release reporting a survey.

DiMarco, B. (2026, March 2; updated 2026, July 13). Legislative tracker: 2026 state AI in education bills. FutureEd, Georgetown University. https://www.future-ed.org/legislative-tracker-2026-state-ai-in-education-bills/ Used for: FutureEd was tracking 77 bills across 27 states addressing AI in classroom instruction during the 2026 session (deck slide 2; script; admin one-pager). We cite FutureEd's count and definition only; other trackers count differently.

Maryland General Assembly. (2026). Senate Bill 720: Education, Artificial Intelligence, Guidelines, Professional Development, and Collaborative (Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act) (Chapter 634, 2026 Laws of Maryland). Approved by the Governor May 26, 2026; effective June 1, 2026. https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Chapters_noln/CH_634_sb0720e.pdf Used for: statewide teacher AI training in a train-the-trainer model "with the intent of training educators by July 1, 2027"; teachers compensated with time, money, or recertification credits and not charged to attend; each local school system designates an AI coordinator; the state publishes a rubric that local systems use to review and select AI tools (deck slides 2 and 3; script; admin one-pager). The July 2027 date is stated intent, not a penalty deadline.

McDonald, C. J. (2026, July 7). Virginia to establish guidance for artificial intelligence in schools [Client alert on HB 1186 and SB 394, effective July 1, 2026]. Williams Mullen. https://www.williamsmullen.com/insights/news/legal-news/virginia-establish-guidance-artificial-intelligence-schools Used for: Virginia's 2026 laws direct statewide AI guidance with resources and training for teachers, require local school boards to adopt aligned policies, and require pilot divisions to include professional development on AI literacy and responsible use (deck slide 2; admin one-pager). A law firm summary, cited as such.

Ohio Department of Education and Workforce. (n.d.). AI in Ohio's education [State agency guidance page]. https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/AI-in-Ohio-s-Education Used for: each school district, community school, and STEM school must adopt an AI policy no later than July 1, 2026 (deck slide 2; admin one-pager). Cited for policy adoption only; the page names no teacher-training requirement.

Further reading: five worth your time

  • Your state department of education's AI guidance: the fastest way to learn what your own state expects of staff training. Search your department's site for "artificial intelligence."
  • FutureEd's legislative tracker (linked above): updated as the session moves; read its definition of what it counts.
  • The enrolled text of Maryland's SB 720 (linked above): plain enough to read in one sitting, and a useful model for what a state training requirement can look like.
  • Kit 1 and Kit 2 references in this series: the privacy rule and the four-part prompt formula this session reuses are sourced there.
  • Your district's acceptable-use and student-data policies: read them before you share a game file with students.
