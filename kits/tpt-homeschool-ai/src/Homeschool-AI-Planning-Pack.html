Homeschool AI Planning Pack

You teach, plan, grade, keep records, and run the field trips — usually in the hours everyone else calls "free time." This pack puts AI to work on the drafting side of all of it: twenty subject-organized prompts, a fill-in unit study builder, a weekly rhythm planner, and the family data-safety habit on one page. AI drafts; you — the person who actually knows your child — decide.

The two rules that make every page safe
Describe the learner, never the child — no names, ever.
And grown-ups hold the keyboard: chatbots are built for 13+.

How to use this pack

  • 1 · Pick the prompt that matches today's job, fill the [BRACKETS] — age, subject, topic, quirks like "loves horses, allergic to worksheets."
  • 2 · Treat the draft as a first draft. Reply to sharpen it: "shorter days," "more hands-on," "she already knows this part." Three replies beats starting over.
  • 3 · Keep a "prompts that worked" page. When one nails your family's style, save it with the brackets blank — that's your personal curriculum machine.

Language arts

1 · Copywork & dictation, from your own unit

"Create five copywork sentences and two dictation passages for a [AGE]-year-old from our unit on [TOPIC]. Beautiful, meaningful sentences — no filler. Order easiest to hardest."

2 · Narration questions for any book

"We just read [CHAPTER/BOOK]. Give me six narration prompts for a [AGE]-year-old, from 'tell me what happened' to 'what would you have done' — plus one drawing prompt for a day when words won't come."

3 · The gentle writing conference

"Here is my child's writing, typed by me with the name removed: [PASTE]. List three genuine strengths and the ONE next skill worth teaching — not every error. Suggest how to teach it in ten minutes with an example, not a lecture."

Math

4 · Word problems about their world

"Write eight math word problems practicing [SKILL] for a [AGE]-year-old, set in [THEIR CURRENT OBSESSION — dragons, soccer, baking]. Two should be multi-step. Answer key with the thinking shown."

5 · Explain it three ways

"Explain [MATH CONCEPT] to a [AGE]-year-old three ways: with objects we have at home, with a drawing I can copy, and with a story. Flag the most common misconception kids have here and how I'd spot it."

6 · The five-minute fact drill, disguised

"Give me three no-materials games that drill [MATH FACTS] for a [AGE]-year-old — one for the car, one for dinner prep, one for a walk. Each under five minutes."

Science & social studies

7 · The kitchen experiment

"Design a safe experiment about [CONCEPT] using normal household items, for a [AGE]-year-old: a question to ask before, numbered steps, what should happen, why it happens in kid words, and one 'what if we changed…' extension."

8 · Living-books hunt

"Suggest ten engaging children's books (stories and narrative nonfiction, not textbooks) about [TOPIC] for a [AGE]-year-old, with one line on why each is good. I'll check our library's catalog — flag any that might be hard to find."

9 · The timeline that builds itself

"For our study of [HISTORICAL PERIOD], list 10 kid-significant events with dates and one vivid sentence each, ordered for a wall timeline. Include two events about ordinary children of that time."

The family data-safety habit

Every prompt in this pack already follows it. Keep it when you write your own:

  • 1 · Strip identity. No names, no photos, no "my daughter Ella" — not yours, not co-op kids'.
  • 2 · Describe the learner. Age, level, interests, what they struggle with: "a 9-year-old who reads above grade but resists writing."
  • 3 · Keep records offline. Portfolios, evaluations, and anything with your child's name live in your own files — AI can format a blank template; you fill it privately.

The ten-second test: could a stranger reading this prompt identify your child? If yes, generalize it. The AI never needed to know who — only what kind of help.
