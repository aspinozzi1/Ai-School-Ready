Homeschool Attendance Sheet

A monthly grid to mark each school day, a year-at-a-glance summary against your own target, and a simple subject log — the paper record most states ask homeschooling families to keep.

Before you use this page
Attendance rules differ by state and change.
Check your own state's homeschool statute, or your state's page from the Home School Legal Defense Association, before you rely on this for a legal requirement.

Ten rows covers most school-year calendars; add a sticky note for an eleventh or twelfth month if your family schools year-round.

Where you stand against your own target

Divide your running total by your target and match the percentage below — a plain math check, not a rule from any state.

Behind (under 80% of target for time elapsed) On track (80–100%) Ahead (over 100%)

Behind isn't an emergency this early in the year — it's a nudge to add a school day or two before the gap grows. Compare the percentage of the year elapsed against the percentage of your target logged, not just the raw numbers.

Simple subject log

One line per week — a quick record of what got covered, useful for a portfolio review or your own peace of mind. Twelve rows covers one grading period; photocopy a fresh page for the next one.
