Business idea starter

Every business starts with a problem somebody noticed. Four quick activities, no help required — work through them in any order, then read the sentence you end up with out loud. If it makes you want to actually try it, you're onto something.

Activity A · The problem hunt

List three everyday annoyances or needs you notice around you — at home, at school, in your neighborhood. For each one, jot down a product or service that could fix it.

1
The annoyance or need
My idea to fix it
2
The annoyance or need
My idea to fix it
3
The annoyance or need
My idea to fix it

Activity B · Community-need checklist

Check any of these that neighbors or family often ask for help with — real needs make real businesses.

Yard work (weeding, raking, watering)
Pet care (walking, feeding, sitting)
Tech help (setting up devices, printers)
Watching younger kids
Organizing (closets, garages, toy bins)
Baking or cooking (with adult help)
Car washing
Making or fixing things (crafts, repairs)
Errands (mail, packages, small trips)
Something else you notice — write it in

What makes an idea worth trying:

  • Someone besides you actually wants it — not just your parents being nice.
  • You can make or do it with what you already have, or a few dollars of supplies.
  • You'd still want to do it even on a day when nobody's watching.

Activity C · Sell it in one sentence

Pick your favorite idea from above. Try selling it in one sentence — three times. Each try should get shorter and clearer than the last.

Try 1
The whole idea — every detail you can fit.
Try 2 — shorter
Cut it down. What actually matters?
Try 3 — the one you'd actually say out loud
One sentence. If a stranger heard only this, would they get it?

Activity D · Rate your idea

Before you tell anyone about it, check it against these four questions. All four checked means it's ready to say out loud.

Would someone besides my family actually pay for this?
Can I make or do it with what I already have?
Would I still want to do it on a day nobody's watching?
Is there something about it that's mine — not a copy of someone else's?

For the grown-up: Want more ideas branching off what your kid already wrote? Try this optional prompt — you run it, not them.

"A [AGE]-year-old wrote down these problem/idea pairs: [PASTE WHAT THEY WROTE IN ACTIVITY A]. Suggest four more kid-run business ideas in the same spirit — each needing under $10 of supplies and sellable to family, friends, or neighbors. One line each, no explanation needed."

Same rule as always: no name, photo, school, or street — describe the kid generically, never who. Chatbots are for 13 and up, so this one's for you to run, not them.
