Homeschool AI planning prompts

Five ready-to-run prompts for the planning that eats homeschool evenings. Fill the brackets, review the draft like the parent-teacher you are, keep what works.

Before you type anything
Describe the learner, never the child.
No names, ever — and grown-ups hold the keyboard.

1 · The weekly plan

"Draft a 5-day homeschool plan for a [GRADE-LEVEL] learner covering [SUBJECTS/UNITS]. One main activity and one review activity per subject per day. Keep each day's total to about [X] hours."

2 · The unit study starter

"Build a one-week unit study on [TOPIC] for a [AGE] learner: a hands-on activity, a living-book style read-aloud description, one writing prompt, and one question to research further."

3 · Leveled reading passage

"Write a 200-word passage about [TOPIC] at a [READING LEVEL], with three comprehension questions after it — one recall, one inference, one opinion."

4 · The sick-parent day

"Give me a low-effort, mostly independent learning day for a [AGE] learner on [SUBJECT]: worksheets or activities that need almost no adult supervision, using only things a home already has."

5 · The field trip prep

"We're visiting [PLACE]. Give me five things to look for while we're there and three follow-up questions to discuss afterward, matched to a [AGE] learner."

How to use this sampler

  • Run it once, then edit, not once and done. The first draft is a starting point — swap in your kid's actual interests, your actual books, your actual week.
  • Keep the good ones. Start a running doc of prompts that worked; next month's planning gets faster every time you reuse one.
  • One prompt, one job. Ask for the weekly plan and the unit starter separately rather than everything at once — shorter asks get sharper answers.

The family data-safety habit

Every prompt above describes the learner, never the child — no names, ages tied to a name, or identifying detail. "A curious 8-year-old who loves dinosaurs" is plenty; a name and a school never are. And the grown-up always holds the keyboard: AI drafts ideas for you to adapt, never a conversation your child has alone.
