The 10-minute AI habit

You don't learn AI in a summer workshop. You learn it ten minutes at a time, on real tasks, until it's just how you work. This sheet is the whole system: one calendar slot, one task a week, one running doc of prompts that worked. By winter break you'll have a personal toolkit no PowerPoint could have given you.

The habit, in three moves

  • 1 · Same slot every week. Put ten minutes on your calendar now — same day, same time, attached to something you already do (planning period, Friday coffee). If it isn't scheduled, it isn't a habit; it's an intention.
  • 2 · One task, not "learn AI." Each week, pick a single real task from the ladder below — something you were going to do anyway. Run it with AI. Compare against how long it usually takes.
  • 3 · Save what worked. Keep one running doc titled "Prompts that worked." When a prompt saves you twenty minutes, paste it in with a one-line note. This doc becomes the most valuable planning file you own.
The rule rides along, every week
Never put student personally identifiable information into a public AI tool.
Describe the need, not the child.

The eight-week starter ladder

Climb in order or jump around — each rung is one week's ten minutes, with a prompt to start from.

WeekTry this task
1 · Parent message"Draft a warm note to families about [TOPIC]. Under 120 words, one clear action step." Rewrite it in your voice; notice what you kept.
2 · Level a text"Rewrite this passage at a [GRADE] reading level, keeping the key vocabulary: [PASTE]." Compare it to the original like an editor.
3 · Exit tickets"Write five exit-ticket questions on [TOPIC] for [GRADE], from recall to 'explain why.' Include an answer key."
4 · The email you're dreadingHave AI draft the reply you keep postponing (no names in the prompt — describe the situation generally). Edit until it sounds like you.
5 · Differentiate one task"Create three versions of this activity — more scaffolding, on level, extension: [PASTE]." You match versions to students; it never knows who they are.
6 · Rubric in minutes"Create a 4-level rubric for [ASSIGNMENT], criteria: [3–4 CRITERIA], written so a [GRADE] student can self-assess."
7 · Sub plans, before you need them"Write a one-page substitute plan for [GRADE/TOPIC]: bell-ringer, main activity with numbered directions, early-finisher task." File it. Future-you says thanks.
8 · Your reusable templateTake your best prompt so far and turn the specifics into [BRACKETS]. That's a template you'll reuse all year — the whole game in one move.
