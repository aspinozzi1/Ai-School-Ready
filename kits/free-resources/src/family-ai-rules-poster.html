Our family AI rules
1
Grown-ups hold the keyboard. Chatbots are built for ages 13+. Kids watch, ask, and decide with us — we type.
2
AI guesses; it doesn't know. We check important things somewhere real before we believe them.
3
Our names and photos stay ours. No names, faces, addresses, or family stories go into AI tools. Ever.
4
AI helps us think — it doesn't think for us. Drafts and ideas are fine. Your work, your words, your decisions.
5
We talk about the weird stuff. If AI says something confusing, wrong, or upsetting, we bring it to each other. Nobody's in trouble.

Why these five rules, and how to talk about them

Signing a poster together beats a lecture. Go rule by rule, ask "when might this one matter?", and let your kids give the examples — they'll surprise you. Here's the thinking behind each rule, in words that hold up to follow-up questions.

  • 1 · Grown-ups hold the keyboard. This isn't our rule — it's the tools' rule (13+), and pediatricians agree. Framing it like the deep end of the pool keeps it about readiness, not punishment: "not yet" instead of "never."
  • 2 · AI guesses. The single most useful thing a child can know about AI. It predicts likely words; it states wrong things confidently. "Let's check" is the habit that makes every future AI tool safer for them.
  • 3 · Our names stay ours. Whatever goes into a free AI tool may be stored beyond your control. Model it out loud: "I'm asking about a seven-year-old who loves dinosaurs — the AI doesn't need to know it's you."
  • 4 · AI doesn't think for us. The homework-and-beyond rule. Using AI to brainstorm or check is a skill; pasting its words as your own is skipping the learning. Your work, your words.
  • 5 · We talk about the weird stuff. The safety net under everything. Kids who expect a calm conversation report the strange thing; kids who expect trouble hide it.

Make it stick

Post it where screens live. Revisit when something changes — a new tool at school, a birthday near 13, a sibling old enough to ask. Re-sign each school year; the re-signing conversation is the real product.
