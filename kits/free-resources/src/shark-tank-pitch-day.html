Shark Tank Pitch Day

Turn a business idea into a thirty-second pitch a real judge could listen to. Plan it below, practice it out loud three times, then bring it to whoever's playing shark today — a parent, a teacher, a sibling, a stuffed animal with strong opinions.

1 · My business name
2 · What I'm selling
One sentence — what it is, and what it does.
3 · Why it's great
What makes someone pick yours over anyone else's?
4 · My price
And one reason that price is fair.
5 · My 30-second pitch
Write it out, then say it out loud until it feels natural — not memorized.

Judge's rubric

For the grown-up (or friend, or sibling) playing judge: circle a star for each row after the pitch.

The grown-up coach page

Your child does the thinking; you do the typing. These AI prompts turn Pitch Day into a real dress rehearsal — run them, read the results together, and let your kid practice against a few gentle challenges before the real pitch.

Judge questions (after question 5)

"A [AGE]-year-old is about to pitch a home business idea called [BUSINESS NAME] that sells [WHAT THEY'RE SELLING] for [PRICE]. Write five gentle judge questions a kind 'Shark Tank' judge might ask a kid this age — encouraging, not tricky, each one line."

Practice objections (optional, for confident pitchers)

"Pretend you are a friendly customer at a kid's pitch table. I'll read you a [AGE]-year-old's pitch for [BUSINESS NAME]; respond with one honest but gentle objection a real customer might raise (price, need, or 'why yours?'), so they can practice answering it. Keep it kind — the pitcher is a kid."

The safety frame (say it once, mean it always):

  • You hold the keyboard — chatbots are for 13 and up, and this stays true on Pitch Day too.
  • Your child's name, photo, school, and street never go into an AI tool — say "an 8-year-old," never who.
  • If Pitch Day ever goes online (a class video, a recorded pitch), the grown-up runs it, start to finish.
