AI quick-start for homeschool parents

You're the teacher, the principal, and the curriculum department. AI can't replace any of that — but it can draft the time-eating parts in minutes: the week plan, the unit study, the leveled reading, the quiz. Ten prompts, safe as written. Fill the [BRACKETS], review like the parent-teacher you are, and keep what works.

The one habit, from day one
Describe the learner, never the child.
"My 8-year-old who loves horses and reads above grade level" — no names, ever. And you hold the keyboard: chatbots are 13+.

Planning

1 · This week, drafted

"You are an experienced homeschool educator. Draft a weekly plan for a [AGE]-year-old covering [SUBJECTS]. We school [DAYS/HOURS]. Include one hands-on activity per day, a read-aloud slot, and margin for rabbit holes. Format: a simple table I can print."

2 · The unit study builder

"Build a 2-week unit study on [TOPIC — volcanoes, ancient Egypt, birds] for a [AGE]-year-old: books to request from the library, one experiment or project per week, writing prompts, math connections, and a simple final project. Assume a real kitchen-table budget."

3 · Level any reading

"Rewrite this passage for a child reading at roughly [LEVEL], keeping the key facts and vocabulary I've marked: [PASTE]. Then add three discussion questions, from 'what happened' to 'what do you think.'"

4 · The quiz that makes itself

"Create a friendly 8-question check on [TOPIC WE JUST FINISHED] for a [AGE]-year-old: mixed formats, one silly question to keep it fun, answer key with a note on what each miss suggests we review."

5 · Spelling & word lists

"Make a 10-word spelling list for a [AGE]-year-old from our unit on [TOPIC], easiest to hardest, plus a sentence for each and one game to practice them that needs no materials."

The days that need saving

6 · The field trip, squeezed dry

"We're visiting [PLACE] this week. Give me: three things to notice there for a [AGE]-year-old, five questions to ask during, and one follow-up activity at home. Keep it light — it's a trip, not a test."

7 · The sick-parent day

"I'm under the weather. Plan a low-supervision learning day for a [AGE]-year-old using only books, paper, and things in a normal house. Independent blocks of 20–30 minutes, written as instructions my child can follow."

8 · Explain it better than I just did

"Explain [CONCEPT — borrowing in subtraction, why seasons happen] to a [AGE]-year-old three different ways: an everyday analogy, a 5-minute kitchen demonstration, and a story. I'll pick the one that fits my kid."

The record-keeping you keep postponing

9 · The portfolio entry

"Turn these rough notes about what we covered this month into a clean portfolio/records entry: [PASTE NOTES]. Organized by subject, factual tone, suitable for our state's homeschool documentation."

10 · The co-op class you volunteered for

"Plan a 45-minute co-op class on [TOPIC] for [NUMBER] kids ages [RANGE]: a 5-minute hook, one main activity with cheap materials, and a wrap-up that works with mixed ages. Include a supply list."
