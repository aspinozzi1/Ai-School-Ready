Talking to parents about AI

Families aren't against AI in school; they're against not knowing.

"Is my child's information safe?"

Lead with the rule, word for word: our staff never put student personally identifiable information into public AI tools. Not names, not grades tied to a name, not anything that could identify a child. Teachers use AI for their own prep with placeholders, and add the personal parts themselves, offline.

"Is AI teaching my child instead of the teacher?"

No. AI drafts, the teacher decides. Teachers use it the way they use any prep tool: to save time on drafts and paperwork so more of their energy goes to students. Nothing AI-drafted reaches a student or family without a human reviewing it first.

"Is my child using AI to cheat?"

Be honest: students everywhere are already trying these tools; pretending otherwise helps no one. What changes is clarity: teachers say per assignment where AI is allowed, expect honesty about how it was used, and design work that makes the thinking visible.

"Will AI decide my child's grades?"

No. AI never grades students here. A teacher may use AI to draft a rubric or feedback language, but judgment about a child's work belongs to the person who knows the child. That line does not move.

"Should my child be using AI at home?"

Point to the tool's own terms first: most AI chatbots set minimum ages and expect parental consent for minors. At home, the same habits we teach at school apply: never share personal information, treat AI answers as a starting point to check, and keep the thinking your own.

Tone that works: say what you do, what you never do, and who to ask when a family has a concern.
