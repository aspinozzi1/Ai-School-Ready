Why this list, and what to type instead

Public AI chatbots have no data agreement with your district, so student details belong in your gradebook, never in the prompt. The good news: the safe version works just as well — the AI never needed to know who, only what kind of help.

The three moves

  • 1 · Strip identity. No names, initials, IDs, photos, or one-of-a-kind details.
  • 2 · Generalize. "A 4th grader" · "several students" · "a parent" · "my third period."
  • 3 · Describe the need, not the child. The reading level, the behavior pattern, the format you want back — that's all the AI ever needed.

Instead of typing that… type this

Risky promptSafe prompt (works just as well)
"Write a comment for Maya Chen, who got a 61% and has ADHD.""Write an encouraging progress comment for a student scoring in the low 60s who struggles with sustained focus."
"Email Jayden's mom about him pushing another student at recess.""Draft a factual, non-judgmental note to a parent about a physical incident at recess, with a partnership tone and a clear next step."
"Simplify this reading for my student with an IEP, Marcus.""Rewrite this passage at a 3rd grade reading level, keeping the key vocabulary."
"Make a seating chart: Ava can't sit near Liam because…"Keep seating charts out of AI entirely — that's judgment about specific kids. It was never a formatting problem.
Before you press enter
Could anyone who knows your school tell which child this is?
If yes — strip, generalize, describe the need.

Names go back in after the draft comes back — into your own email, never into the tool.
