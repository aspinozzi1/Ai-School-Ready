Before you start: what "ABC" means

ABC stands for antecedent, behavior, consequence — what happened right before a behavior, what the behavior actually was, and what happened right after it. It is the standard structure behavior analysts and special educators use to describe an incident before a team decides what, if anything, to do about it (Cooper, Heron, & Heward, Applied Behavior Analysis, Pearson). Write down what you saw and heard, plainly and without judgment — "raised his voice and left his seat," not "was defiant." A description a colleague could picture without having been in the room is the goal on every line.

What the data suggests we try next: the zero-technology check

No chart, no AI, no login — match what you're seeing in the pattern summary to the row below for one reasonable next step to bring to the team.

What the pattern showsWhat it usually meansA reasonable next step
Same antecedent almost every time (a transition, a specific task, a specific peer)Something about that trigger is hard for this student right nowBring the pattern to the team; consider a support built around that specific moment
Same consequence almost every time (adult attention, escape from a task, peer reaction)That outcome may be what the behavior is achieving for the studentDiscuss with the team whether the same outcome could be reached a different way

The AI Pattern Summary page

Turn a week of de-identified ABC entries into a plain-language pattern summary. Use a student number only. Never paste a name, a class roster, or a diagnosis.

Worked example — Student #22, five ABC entries this week: (1) transition to math, called out, teacher redirected; (2) transition to math, left seat, sent to hallway; (3) independent reading, quiet, no incident; (4) transition to math, called out, peers laughed; (5) transition to lunch, no incident.

Pattern: three of five entries cluster around the transition into math.
What this is not: not a diagnosis, not a claim about why math specifically is hard, and not a behavior plan — those stay the team's call once more data or a formal assessment is in.

Copy-paste prompt: this week's entries in one paragraph

"Here are this week's ABC (antecedent-behavior-consequence) entries for one student, by student number only: [PASTE THE ANTECEDENT, BEHAVIOR, AND CONSEQUENCE FROM EACH ROW — NO NAME, NO CLASS ROSTER, NO DIAGNOSIS]. In plain language, describe any pattern across the antecedents and any pattern across the consequences. Do not suggest a cause, a diagnosis, or a behavior plan — describe only what the data shows."

The chatbot describes a pattern from entries you already wrote. Whether that pattern means anything, and what to do about it, stay the team's decisions — every time, for every student.

What this pack is not

  • Not a functional behavior assessment (FBA). A full FBA follows your district's official process and is typically conducted or overseen by a qualified professional; these sheets are the informal, day-to-day recording that can feed into one.
  • Not a behavior intervention plan (BIP). This pack describes; it never prescribes what to do about a behavior.
  • Not a diagnosis. It never names or implies a disorder.

Filing this pack

  • File by student number in a locked cabinet or a password-protected folder, matching your district's storage policy for behavior records.
  • Shred or securely delete working sheets once they've served their purpose, per your district's retention schedule.
