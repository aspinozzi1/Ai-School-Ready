Keeping a completed sheet confidential

This page is practical handling guidance, not a statement of the law. Your district's own policy and FERPA are the actual authority — check both before you set up a system.

  • Store completed sheets in a locked cabinet, a case manager's binder, or a password-protected folder — never loose in a take-home folder, a mailbox, or face-up on a desk where any visitor can read it.
  • A guest teacher or a specialist covering the room gets the accommodations-only quick-reference card further in this pack — never the full snapshot with goals and services attached.
  • Completed sheets come back to the case manager at the end of the year and are not sent home in a cumulative folder or a keepsake binder.
  • Ask your district's special education office where completed sheets fit in the official records-retention schedule before you build a permanent system around them.

Snapshot — compact layout

Fits most students in one page. Use a student number on any copy that leaves the case manager's hands, and the student's name only on the one copy that stays in the locked file.

Keep this page current

  • Re-copy the snapshot after every IEP review — a snapshot built from a plan that changed six months ago sends staff the wrong accommodations.
  • Date the bottom corner in pencil so anyone picking it up can tell at a glance whether it is this year's version.
  • Check it against the actual IEP once a semester, not just at the start of the year — plans change mid-year more often than paperwork gets updated.

Snapshot — extended layout

More room per section, for a student with several related-service providers, a behavior support plan, or a health accommodation the room needs to know about.

From accommodation to classroom practice

This page works differently from every other AI page we build. Everywhere else, de-identified data is safe to paste. Here, the sheet itself holds identified information, so the rule is stricter.

The one rule on this page
A completed snapshot sheet never goes into a chatbot.
Only a generic accommodation type does — no student, no grade-plus-need combination, nothing traceable.

Turn a generic accommodation into a classroom routine

"A student needs extended time and directions broken into smaller steps. Suggest three practical ways to offer that in a [GRADE LEVEL] [SUBJECT] classroom of about 25 students, without singling anyone out in front of peers."

Plan for a hard moment without naming anyone

"A student benefits from a quiet space and a break card when overwhelmed. Suggest two low-key ways a busy classroom teacher could offer this without drawing the class's attention."

The chatbot only ever sees a need that could belong to any student anywhere. The name, the grade-plus-need combination, and the plan for one specific child stay with you.

What this pack is not

  • Not an IEP. It is a working summary of one, built from a document the team has already finalized.
  • Not a legal document and not a substitute for reading the actual IEP before you teach or support this student.
  • Not a compliance record. Follow your district's official process for anything that must be filed or signed.
