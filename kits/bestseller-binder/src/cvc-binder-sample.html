How to use this unit

Print the practice pages once and slide them into sleeves — students write with a dry-erase marker and the binder lasts all year. Teach the unit, practice daily, then give the ten-word probe on the data sheet. Take the score to the Growth Eval page and it tells you whether to hold or move.

Why the sequence is printed here

So you can slot this into whatever phonics program you already use instead of guessing what it assumes. Short a comes first because it is the most common short vowel in early decodable words, and e comes last because e and i are the pair students most often confuse — separating them reduces that collision. Every word in this unit is a true CVC word: one consonant, one short vowel, one consonant.
Structure follows the IES practice guide's two strong-evidence recommendations for K–3: link speech sounds to letters, and teach students to decode, write, and recognize words. Full citation on the last page.

AI Growth Eval · one student

Find the score from the data sheet. Read across. No technology needed for any of this.
0–4
The sound isn't secure yet.
  • Drop back to letter–sound work with just a — three minutes daily.
  • Use two-sound words (at, am) before three-sound words.
  • Re-probe in one week with the same ten words.
5–7
Blending is emerging but not automatic.
  • Stay in the unit and keep the Build-It step — don't skip to writing.
  • Time one page: if accuracy is fine, the gap is speed, not the sound.
  • Re-probe in one week; move at 8 twice in a row.
8–10
Short a is solid. Move.
  • Start Unit 2 (short i); keep short a alive in mixed review.
  • Spot-check short a again in three weeks.

How this binder is built

What we based it on, what we are claiming, and what we are not.

The instructional structure

Each unit links speech sounds to letters and then asks the student to decode, build, and write the same words. That mirrors the two strong-evidence recommendations in the IES/What Works Clearinghouse practice guide for K–3 foundational skills: "Develop awareness of the segments of sounds in speech and how they link to letters" and "Teach students to decode words, analyze word parts, and write and recognize words."

The 8-of-10 move-on signal

Mastery criteria in phonics commonly sit between 80% and 100% accuracy, with 90% often cited. We ask for 80% on two consecutive probes rather than 90% once, because repeating the criterion is what separates a good day from a secure skill. Adjust it to match whatever your school already uses — the data sheet works either way.

What we are not claiming

This binder is not aligned to, affiliated with, or a substitute for any named phonics program, and it is not a diagnostic assessment. It is practice material and a ten-word progress check. Every word in Unit 1 is a true short-a CVC word — one consonant, one short vowel, one consonant — with no blends or digraphs, so the probe measures the one thing it says it measures.
