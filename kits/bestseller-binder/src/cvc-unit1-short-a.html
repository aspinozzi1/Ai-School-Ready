What is in this unit

Six practice pages, a ten-word probe, a four-week data sheet, and two pages that turn the scores into next steps. Print once and slide the practice pages into sleeves — students write with a dry-erase marker and the unit lasts all year.

Why the sequence is printed here

So you can slot this into whatever phonics program you already use instead of guessing what it assumes. Short a comes first because it appears in more early decodable words than any other short vowel; e comes last because e and i are the pair students most often confuse, and separating them reduces that collision.
Structure follows the IES practice guide's two strong-evidence recommendations for K–3: link speech sounds to letters, and teach students to decode, write and recognise words. Full citation on the last page.

Teacher note
Tapping the sounds before writing separates two different problems. A student who taps three sounds correctly but writes the wrong letter has a letter–sound gap. A student who cannot tap three sounds at all has a segmenting gap, and needs the sounds before the spelling. Note which one you saw — the Growth Eval asks.

AI Growth Eval · one student

Find the score from the data sheet. Read across. No technology needed for any of this.
0–4
The sound is not secure yet.
  • Drop back to letter–sound work with just a — three minutes daily.
  • Use the sound-box page before the writing pages.
  • Re-probe in one week with the same ten words.
5–7
Blending is emerging but not automatic.
  • Stay in the unit; keep the Build-It step rather than jumping to writing.
  • Compare Practice 1 and Practice 2 — if only the no-picture page slipped, the pictures were decoding for them.
  • Re-probe in one week; move at 8 twice in a row.
8–10
Short a is solid. Move.
  • Start Unit 2 (short i); keep short a alive in mixed review.
  • Spot-check short a again in three weeks.

For the teacher

How this unit is built

What we based it on, what we are claiming, and what we are not.

The instructional structure

Each unit links speech sounds to letters and then asks the student to decode, build and write the same words. That mirrors the two strong-evidence recommendations in the IES / What Works Clearinghouse practice guide for K–3 foundational skills: "Develop awareness of the segments of sounds in speech and how they link to letters" and "Teach students to decode words, analyze word parts, and write and recognize words."

The 8-of-10 move-on signal

Mastery criteria in phonics commonly sit between 80% and 100% accuracy, with 90% often cited. We ask for 80% on two consecutive probes rather than 90% once, because repeating the criterion is what separates a good day from a secure skill. Adjust it to match whatever your school already uses — the data sheet works either way.

What we are not claiming

This unit is not aligned to, affiliated with, or a substitute for any named phonics program, and it is not a diagnostic assessment. It is practice material and a ten-word progress check. Every target word in this unit is a true short-a CVC word — one consonant, one short vowel, one consonant — with no blends or digraphs, verified by an automated check before publication, so the probe measures the one thing it says it measures.
