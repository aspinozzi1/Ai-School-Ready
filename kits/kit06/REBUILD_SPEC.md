# Kit 6 rebuild spec — "The message before the message"

Owner approved this thesis 2026-08-15. Rebuild from scratch on this spec.

## Why the old kit had to go

The old Kit 6 taught teachers to draft parent messages faster: three makeovers, a
tone-repair kit, a note batch. That is the most crowded square in the entire
AI-for-teachers market. Every competitor ships parent-email templates. Speed of
drafting is not a thing a school will pay for twice, and it is not what actually goes
wrong in family communication.

## The thesis

**AI's real value here is not writing your hard message. It is making the ordinary,
specific, positive contact happen at a volume nobody sustains by hand, which is exactly
what buys you credibility before you need it. The hard message you still write
yourself.**

This inverts the category. Everyone else automates the difficult conversation. We
automate the easy volume and deliberately protect the difficult one.

## The argument, in order

1. **The last-contact test.** Open with a personal audit, not a lecture. Pick one
   student, ideally one you find difficult. When did that family last hear from you
   when nothing was wrong? The room's silence is the opening. Nobody presents at this
   moment; the presenter waits.
2. **The asymmetry.** If the only contact a family gets is bad news, every message from
   school arrives as an accusation, and the hard conversation starts from a deficit you
   created without meaning to.
3. **The evidence that this is not soft.** Kraft & Rogers ran a field experiment with
   weekly individualized teacher-to-parent messages: failure to earn course credit fell
   from 15.8% to 9.3%, a 41% reduction. State the scope out loud every time: a summer
   credit-recovery program, and the study tested a bundle (individualized content,
   weekly frequency, improvement framing), so no single ingredient is proven to be the
   active one. Do not call personalization "the mechanism."
4. **Why it doesn't happen.** Teachers work about 54 hours a week (Merrimack/EdWeek)
   and roughly two of those go to communicating with parents. The routine positive
   contact is the first thing that dies. This is a capacity problem, not a caring
   problem, and capacity is the one thing a tool can actually touch.
5. **So this is what AI is for here.** The volume. AI drafts the shape of four short
   notes; the teacher supplies the one specific detail per child that proves somebody
   was looking. Show generic against specific side by side: "Maya is doing well" versus
   "Maya found the mistake in my worked example on Tuesday and said so in front of
   everyone." The second one cannot be generated, and it is the whole point.
6. **The hard message, which you write.** Structure taught plainly: lead with the fact,
   not the feeling; make one ask; promise nothing you cannot deliver; and know when to
   stop typing and pick up the phone. AI's role here is narrowed to one job, a second
   read for tone before you send. Never the words.
7. **The red lines.** No public AI translation of family messages: you cannot review
   what you cannot read. Official and legal documents travel the district's translation
   channels, which is the district's obligation. Keep the law and our rule visibly
   separate: the 2015 ED/DOJ Dear Colleague Letter requires meaningful communication
   with limited-English-proficient parents and says machine translation of essential
   information needs qualified review; it does not itself forbid teachers from using AI
   translation. The classroom rule is ours, stated as ours. Plus the human-only list:
   safety, discipline, grief, and special-education news get a voice, not a message.
8. **Close.** Commitments, First 48 Hours, exit ticket.

## The exemplar: ONE artifact, followed to completion

Ms. Rivera's Friday ten minutes. One routine, shown through every lab step, never
switching to a different scenario mid-lab.

- **What she starts from:** four families on her list, and the fact that three of them
  have not heard from her since conferences.
- **The prompt she types:** shown in the full-width chat window, color-coded ROLE /
  TASK / CONTEXT / FORMAT with the legend chips.
- **What came back:** four warm, competent, interchangeable notes. Beige. Useful shape,
  no proof anyone was looking.
- **What she adds:** one true specific detail per child, from her own week.
- **The finished product:** the four notes as the families actually receive them, word
  for word, with the specific detail visible in each. Ten minutes, four families.
- **The contrast beat:** the one hard message she had to send that week, which she
  wrote herself, shown beside the routine so the room sees the line.

## The lab (protected, >= 15 min)

1. Name four real families who have not heard from you when nothing was wrong.
2. Draft the shape (the prompt is on the handout).
3. Add the one true detail per child. This step is the session.
4. Write the hard message you have been putting off, yourself, and run one AI second
   read for tone only.

They leave with four notes ready to send and one hard message drafted.

## What is deliberately CUT

- The three makeovers as the spine of the session.
- The tone-repair kit as a taught framework (a lighter version can survive inside the
  hard-message segment, but it is not a system with a name).
- Any framing that positions AI as the author of a difficult message.
- Language translation, which the owner removed 2026-08-12 and which stays out. The
  only permitted mention is the red line above and the district-channels clause.
