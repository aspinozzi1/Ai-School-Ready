References

APA style. The note beneath each entry says exactly where the kit uses it, so any claim can be traced to its source.

Gallup, & Walton Family Foundation. (2025). Teaching for tomorrow. https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx Used for: teachers who use AI weekly reported saving an estimated 5.9 hours a week (slide 5; prep guide; admin one-pager). This is a survey self-estimate, not a measurement, and the kit says so on the slide, in the script, and here.

Kraft, M. A., & Rogers, T. (2015). The underutilized potential of teacher-to-parent communication: Evidence from a field experiment. Economics of Education Review, 47, 49–63. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272775715000497 Used for: weekly individualized teacher-to-parent messages cut the share of students failing to earn the course credit from 15.8% to 9.3%, a 41% reduction (slide 4; handout; admin one-pager; prep guide). Scope ships with the figure everywhere it appears: the experiment ran in a summer credit-recovery program, so participants were retaking a course they had already failed, and it tested a bundle (individualized content, weekly frequency, improvement framing), so no single ingredient is established as the active one. The kit never describes personalization as "the mechanism," and slide 27 restates the boundary as an honest limit.

Merrimack College, & EdWeek Research Center. (2022). How teachers spend their time: A breakdown. Education Week. https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/how-teachers-spend-their-time-a-breakdown/2022/04 Used for: the typical teacher works about 54 hours a week, about 25 of which is actual teaching time, and "communication with parents or guardians" is one of the eleven task categories the survey measured (slide 5; prep guide; admin one-pager). The kit does not assign a specific number of hours to family communication, because the published breakdown we could verify does not report one.

U.S. Department of Education, Office of Educational Technology. (2023). Artificial intelligence and the future of teaching and learning: Insights and recommendations. https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/documents/ai-report/ai-report.pdf Used for: the humans-in-the-loop recommendation behind "nothing reaches a family unread" and behind narrowing AI's role on a hard message to a second read (slides 11 and 27; prep guide).

U.S. Department of Justice, Civil Rights Division, & U.S. Department of Education, Office for Civil Rights. (2015). Dear Colleague Letter: English learner students and limited English proficient parents. https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/about/offices/list/ocr/letters/colleague-el-201501.pdf Used for: the district-level obligation that official and legal documents travel district translation channels (slide 12; prep guide FAQ; admin one-pager). The guidance requires meaningful communication with limited-English-proficient parents and says machine translation of essential information must be reviewed by a qualified person; it does not itself forbid teachers from using AI translation. The classroom red line on slide 12 is AI-Ready School's own stricter rule, presented as ours, and slide 12 keeps the two visibly apart.

Further reading: four worth your time

  • Kraft & Rogers (2015) (linked above): the one-sentence study; short, rigorous, and the best case ever made for the small personal message. Read the setting section, not only the abstract.
  • The 2015 ED/DOJ Dear Colleague Letter (linked above): the district's language-access obligation in plain terms; every leadership team should have read it once.
  • Gallup & Walton's Teaching for Tomorrow (linked above): where teachers say they save time with AI, and a useful lesson in reading a self-report as a self-report.
  • The U.S. DoE's AI report (linked above): the humans-in-the-loop framing this whole series builds on.
Our citation promise: every source in this kit was verified against the live publication, existence and claim, before inclusion. Where a figure could not be verified, it was cut rather than softened into a guess. If a link moves, tell us and we'll ship the fix to every member school.
