References

APA style. The note beneath each entry says exactly where the kit uses it, so any claim can be traced to its source.

Gallup, & Walton Family Foundation. (2025). Teaching for tomorrow. https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx Used for: teachers who use AI weekly save about 5.9 hours a week, with communication drafting among common uses (slide 2; prep guide; admin one-pager).

Kraft, M. A., & Rogers, T. (2015). The underutilized potential of teacher-to-parent communication: Evidence from a field experiment. Economics of Education Review, 47, 49–63. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272775715000497 Used for: in a summer program for students retaking a course they had failed, weekly one-sentence individualized messages cut the share failing to earn the credit from 15.8% to 9.3% (41%), with improvement-focused messages most effective; the basis for the positive-note batch (slides 3 and 11; handout; admin one-pager).

Merrimack College, & EdWeek Research Center. (2022). How teachers spend their time: A breakdown. Education Week. https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/how-teachers-spend-their-time-a-breakdown/2022/04 Used for: the ~54-hour work week with under half spent directly teaching; communication lives in the margins (slide 2; prep guide; admin one-pager).

U.S. Department of Education, Office of Educational Technology. (2023). Artificial intelligence and the future of teaching and learning: Insights and recommendations. https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/documents/ai-report/ai-report.pdf Used for: the humans-in-the-loop recommendation behind "nothing reaches a family unread" (slide 7; prep guide).

U.S. Department of Justice, Civil Rights Division, & U.S. Department of Education, Office for Civil Rights. (2015). Dear Colleague Letter: English learner students and limited English proficient parents. https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/about/offices/list/ocr/letters/colleague-el-201501.pdf Used for: the district-level obligation behind the kit's red line that official and legal documents travel district translation channels, never DIY AI translation (slide 15; prep guide FAQ; admin one-pager).

Further reading: four worth your time

  • Kraft & Rogers (2015) (linked above): the one-sentence study; short, rigorous, and the best case ever made for the small personal message.
  • The 2015 ED/DOJ Dear Colleague Letter (linked above): the district's language-access obligation in plain terms; every leadership team should have read it once.
  • Gallup & Walton's Teaching for Tomorrow (linked above): where teachers actually save time with AI, communication included.
  • The U.S. DoE's AI report (linked above): the humans-in-the-loop framing this whole series builds on.
