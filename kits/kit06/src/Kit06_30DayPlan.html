30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session sent real messages; the month makes reviewed, personal, plain-language communication the school's default. About 30 minutes of existing PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

The line leadership holds all month
Families hear the teacher, not the tool: everything reviewed and personalized, hard news by voice, official documents through district translation. Any drift gets a private, kind correction the week it appears.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Batch Check"  Week 2 · 10 min

Round (6 min): each person reports: did your three notes go out, and what happened? Read one reply from a family if you got one (no names). The stories are the fuel; expect at least one that quiets the room.

Fix (3 min): anyone whose batch stalled names the blocker (usually "forgot" or "felt awkward"); the team picks a shared batch day so the habit has a home.

Close (1 min): confirm next week's batch day. This habit is the single highest-leverage thing the kit installed.

Follow-up #2 · "The Message Makeover Swap"  Week 3 · 10 min

Swap (6 min): each teacher brings one real message from the month that felt off or never got sent: the fee reminder, the tense reply, the newsletter nobody answered. Pairs trade, read each other's aloud, and call the repair: warmer, shorter, plainer, or lead with the action. (The recurring "still want help with" situations from the exit tickets make good material too.)

Repair (3 min): each teacher rewrites their message's hardest sentence, warm, plain, and guilt-free, with the tool or by hand, and reads the before and after to their partner.

Close (1 min): each teacher names the repaired message they'll actually send this week.

Follow-up #3 · "Template Harvest"  Week 4 · 10 min

Harvest (6 min): each person posts their best message of the month to the staff doc's Communication section: newsletter shell, difficult-email frame, event reminder, note starters. Name and one line on when to use it.

Steal (3 min): everyone takes one template they didn't write and schedules its first use. Nobody writes the field-trip reminder from scratch again.

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 7 date; bring your calendar, because Kit 7 audits your week.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: ask three teachers what they sent, not whether they liked the PD; sends are the measure
  • Week 2: be ready with the parent answer, word for word: "Teachers may use AI to draft routine messages, and every message is reviewed and personalized by the teacher before it's sent. Sensitive news is always delivered personally."
  • Week 3: sit in on one makeover swap and bring your own hardest message of the month; nothing normalizes the habit faster than leadership repairing a draft in front of teachers
  • Week 4: collect one story for the board: a family reply to a positive note, or a hard conversation that a repaired message made easier; specifics beat adjectives

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
A message goes out that reads machine-madePrivate, kind correction; re-teach the voice pass. One beige blast erodes more trust than ten good notes build.
Someone drafts sensitive news with AICoach privately the week you learn of it: the human-only list is the non-negotiable of this kit. Re-anchor at the next meeting without naming names.
Note batches die by week 3The blocker is almost always no scheduled day. Pick a shared batch day; five minutes, calendared, survives October.
Hot replies still go out the same nightRe-anchor the 24-hour rule and the vent-draft-deleted move at the next meeting: the tool absorbs the heat, and the reply waits for morning unless safety can't.
