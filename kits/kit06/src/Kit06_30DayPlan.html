30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session sent four notes per teacher. The month turns that into a habit with a day on the calendar, and keeps the hard message in human hands. About 30 minutes of existing PLC time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet: four notes sent, the hard message sent or the call made, and next week's ten minutes on the calendar with the next four families named. Facilitator reads exit tickets and collects the "one true detail" answers for follow-up #2. All staff · Facilitator

The line leadership holds all month
AI handles the ordinary contact. The teacher still writes the hard message. Any drift gets a private, kind correction the week it appears.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "The Friday Ten"  Week 2 · 10 min

Round (6 min): each person answers two questions: did your ten minutes happen, and what came back? Read one family reply aloud if you got one, with no names. The replies are the fuel; expect at least one that quiets the room.

Fix (3 min): anyone whose ten minutes did not happen names the blocker. It is almost always "no day on the calendar." Fix it in the meeting, out loud, with a day name, not a good intention.

Close (1 min): confirm each person's day. This is the single highest-leverage thing the kit installed.

Follow-up #2 · "The Detail Round"  Week 3 · 10 min

Round (6 min): going around the table, each person reads one true sentence they sent home this month. Just the sentence, not the note. No commentary, no praise, no discussion between readings; the round itself does the work.

Swap test (3 min): pairs trade sentences and apply the test out loud: could this be about a different child? Anything that passes generic gets rewritten on the spot, with a real moment from this week.

Close (1 min): name the child you have not written about yet. That name goes on next week's four.

Follow-up #3 · "The Hard Message Read"  Week 4 · 10 min

Read (6 min): each teacher brings one hard message they wrote this month, names removed. Partners read each other's against the four rules: is the fact first, is there exactly one ask with a date, was anything promised that cannot be delivered, and should this have been a phone call?

Repair (3 min): each teacher fixes the one rule their message missed. Most misses are rule 1 (a feeling where a fact belonged) and rule 3 (an over-promise made out of kindness).

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 7 date; bring your calendar, because Kit 7 audits your week.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: ask three teachers what they sent, not whether they liked the PD; sends are the measure
  • Week 2: be ready with the parent answer, word for word: "Teachers may use AI to help draft routine positive notes home, and every one is reviewed and personalized by the teacher before it is sent. Messages about a concern are written by the teacher, and sensitive news is always delivered personally."
  • Week 3: sit in on one detail round and read one of your own. Nothing normalizes the habit faster than leadership doing the noticing out loud
  • Week 4: collect one story for the board: a family reply to an ordinary note, or a hard conversation that went better because the family had already heard from that teacher. Specifics beat adjectives

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
The ten minutes has a dayTeachers name the day without checking; the block is recurring on the calendar
The details are realSentences survive the swap test; two out of four are not academic
Hard messages stay humanTeachers describe writing them, not prompting them. The tool's only role is the tone read
The lines holdNo public AI translation of family messages; hard news travels by voice; official documents travel district channels

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Notes go out with no specific detail in themThe blank got filled with a second generic sentence. Re-teach the swap test at the next meeting and run one round live. One beige blast erodes more trust than ten good notes build.
Someone had AI write a difficult messageCoach privately the week you learn of it. This is the non-negotiable of the kit: a message that carries consequences has to be defensible by the person who sent it. Re-anchor at the next meeting without naming names.
The ten minutes dies by week 3The blocker is almost always no scheduled day. Ten minutes, calendared, on a day with a name, survives October. An intention does not.
Teachers only write about the easy kidsName it kindly and structurally: the difficult student goes on the list first, every week. That family is the one where the ordinary note does the most work.
