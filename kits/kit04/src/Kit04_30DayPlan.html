30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session built one rubric, one question bank, and three feedback starters per person; the month turns them into classroom habits. About 30 minutes of existing PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet: rubric out with the assignment, bank fully vetted, one starter used on a real stack. Facilitator reads the exit tickets and collects the best "weak question I caught" stories for follow-up #2. All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Rubric Swap" (10 min, below): colleagues run the hand-it-to-a-student test on each other's rubrics. PLC leads
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "Vetting Bee" (10 min, below): the team vets one member's AI-drafted question bank together and counts the catches. PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "Starter Share" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month: Assessment-section entries in the staff doc, rubrics in student hands, catch stories. Schedule Kit 5, Academic Integrity. PLC leads · Admin
The line leadership holds all month
AI drafts assessment materials. It never grades students, never sees named student work, and never makes decisions about students. Any drift from that line gets a private, kind correction the week it appears.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Rubric Swap"  Week 2 · 10 min

Swap (5 min): pairs trade rubrics (the lab one or a newer one). Each person reads one descriptor aloud and answers as a student would: "do I know exactly what to do to move up a level?" Vague descriptors get flagged, kindly.

Fix (4 min): authors iterate one flagged descriptor on the spot, live or by hand: name the assignment's specifics, describe what the work does.

Close (1 min): improved rubrics go back in front of students; the best one goes in the staff doc's Assessment section.

Follow-up #2 · "Vetting Bee"  Week 3 · 10 min

Setup (1 min): one volunteer projects a fresh AI-drafted question bank on their own upcoming topic, unvetted, on purpose.

Hunt (7 min): the team vets it together: answer key against professional knowledge, distractors checked for accidental truth, reading level, one fair answer per question. Count the catches out loud; celebrate every one.

Close (2 min): the fixed bank goes back to its owner ready to use. The lesson says itself: drafts are cheap, vetting is sacred, and vetting together is fast.

Follow-up #3 · "Starter Share"  Week 4 · 10 min

Round (6 min): each person reads their most-used feedback starter and says how they personalize it before it reaches a student. Steal freely; patterns repeat across subjects more than anyone expects.

Audit (3 min): skim the staff doc's Assessment section: does it have a rubric template, a bank template, and a starter set? Assign one volunteer per gap.

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 5 date; today's rubrics come along.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: ask three teachers what they built, not whether they liked the PD; artifacts are the measure
  • Week 2: be ready with the parent answer, word for word: "No, AI does not grade students here. Teachers use it to draft rubrics and questions, which they review, and every grade and every comment comes from the teacher."
  • Week 3: collect one concrete story for the board: a vetting catch or a rubric students actually used; numbers beat adjectives
  • Week 4: schedule Kit 5 and note who's emerging as assessment-material champions; they're future facilitators

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
Rubrics reach studentsRubrics go out with assignments, in language students quote back
The vetting reflex is realStaff tell catch stories unprompted; no unvetted bank reaches a quiz
Feedback stays humanStarters show up in grading, personalized every time; no comment reaches a student unedited
The red lines holdEveryone, asked cold, can say what AI never does here: grade, see names, decide

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Someone lets AI score student workCoach privately the week you learn of it; re-anchor to the red lines at the next meeting without naming names. This is the one non-negotiable.
Rubrics in the doc read genericRun Rubric Swap on one of them (with the author's blessing) and feed it the assignment's specifics; upgrading a real rubric teaches more than a fresh example.
Banks go out unvettedRun an extra Vetting Bee and let the catches make the argument; assign vetting partners for a month if needed.
Feedback starts sounding machine-madeRe-teach "starters, not stamps": the personal sentence is mandatory. Have the PLC read starters aloud and add the human line together.
