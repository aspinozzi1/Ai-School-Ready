AI for Assessment: Rubrics, Feedback, and Question Banks

A 45–60 minute, self-facilitated session in which every teacher builds a rubric and a question bank for a real upcoming assessment, plus reusable feedback starters. Run by your own coach or teacher-leader; no consultant required.

The stance, and your parent answer
AI drafts assessment materials. It never grades students, never sees student work with names attached, and never makes decisions about students. Every grade and every comment comes from the teacher.

Why this matters now

Teachers work about 54 hours a week, with a median 5 hours on grading and feedback (Merrimack College/EdWeek, 2022), and feedback is among the most powerful influences on achievement measured, averaging a 0.79 effect size (Hattie & Timperley). Yet RAND found only about 15% or fewer of AI-using teachers used AI for writing assessments in 2023–24: the least-tapped, highest-leverage use of the tools. Among teachers who do use AI, 57% say it improves the quality of their grading and feedback (Gallup, 2025). This session closes that gap with the guardrails written down first.

What your staff will be able to do afterward

  • Turn grading notes into 4-level rubrics in student-readable language, tested by the "hand it to a student" bar
  • Build mixed-format question banks with answer keys and misconception-based distractors, then vet every question by hand
  • Draft reusable feedback starters for common patterns, personalized before any student sees them
  • State the red lines cold: AI never grades, never sees named work, never decides about a student
  • Apply the anonymous-excerpt rule: zero identifying details, or describe the pattern instead
  • Add assessment templates to the shared staff prompt library from Kit 2

Where it fits
