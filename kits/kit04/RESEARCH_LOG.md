# Kit 4 — Research log (sources verified 2026-08-07 via live web search)

Kit 4: "AI for Assessment: Rubrics, Feedback, and Question Banks." Every source verified
to exist and support the claim before use. The kit's stance: AI drafts assessment
*materials*; it never grades students or sees student work with names attached.

| # | Source (APA) | Verified claim used in kit | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (1998). Inside the black box: Raising standards through classroom assessment. *Phi Delta Kappan, 80*(2), 139–148. | Formative assessment, backed by ~250 studies, raises achievement when teachers check understanding day-by-day and adapt. | https://kappanonline.org/inside-the-black-box-raising-standards-through-classroom-assessment/ |
| 2 | Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The power of feedback. *Review of Educational Research, 77*(1), 81–112. | Feedback averages an effect size of 0.79 — among the most powerful influences on achievement — but impact varies hugely with feedback type and delivery. | https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/003465430298487 |
| 3 | Panadero, E., Jonsson, A., Pinedo, L., & Fernández-Castilla, B. (2023). Effects of rubrics on academic performance, self-regulated learning, and self-efficacy: A meta-analytic review. *Educational Psychology Review, 35*(4), 113. | Rubric use has a moderate positive effect on academic performance (g = 0.45); smaller pooled effects for self-regulated learning (g = 0.23) and self-efficacy (g = 0.18), both with confidence intervals including zero. | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4 |
| 4 | Merrimack College & EdWeek Research Center. (2022). How teachers spend their time: A breakdown. *Education Week.* | Teachers work ~54 hours/week, under half spent directly teaching; median 5 hours/week on grading and feedback. | https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/how-teachers-spend-their-time-a-breakdown/2022/04 |
| 5 | Steiss, J., et al. (2024). Comparing the quality of human and ChatGPT feedback of students' writing. *Learning and Instruction, 91*, 101894. | Well-trained humans gave higher-quality feedback than ChatGPT on nearly all dimensions; AI feedback still useful for formative early drafts — supports the teacher-reviews-AI-drafts workflow, honestly framed. | https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475224000215 |
| 6 | Elkins, S., et al. (2023). How useful are educational questions generated by large language models? *Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIED 2023).* Springer. | Teachers rated LLM-generated questions 97% topic-relevant and 96% grammatical, usefulness 3.6/4 — with teacher vetting integral. | https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-36336-8_83 |
| 7 | Kaliisa, R., Misiejuk, K., López-Pernas, S., & Saqr, M. (2025). How does artificial intelligence compare to human feedback? A meta-analysis. *Educational Psychology.* | Across 41 studies (4,813 students), no statistically significant difference in learning performance between AI-generated and human-provided feedback. Framed carefully: the teacher still owns judgment. | https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01443410.2025.2553639 |
| 8 | Kaufman, J. H., Woo, A., Eagan, J., Lee, S., & Kassan, E. B. (2025). *Uneven adoption…* (RR-A134-25). RAND. | About one-fifth of teachers used AI to plan ELA/math/science instruction in 2023-24; among the 1,861 who described that use, about a quarter used AI to help assess students and ~400 to create assessments, quizzes, and rubrics. RAND ranks assessment SECOND in prevalence after generating lesson materials. Corrected 2026-08-15 after an external citation audit; the earlier "15% or fewer / least-tapped" reading was wrong. | https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA134-25.html |
| 9 | Gallup & Walton Family Foundation. (2025). *Teaching for tomorrow.* | 57% of AI-using teachers say AI improves the quality of their grading and student feedback. | https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx |

**Claims deliberately avoided (could not verify):** Black & Wiliam's oft-quoted 0.4–0.7
effect-size range (cite only the ~250-studies claim); full author lists for Steiss et
al. and Elkins et al. (verified lead authors; "et al." used); pooled effect size from
Kaliisa et al. beyond the no-significant-difference finding.
