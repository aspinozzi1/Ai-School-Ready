30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

One session changes an afternoon; the month after changes practice. The research on effective PD is consistent: active use, collaboration, and sustained duration are what make training stick. This plan needs about 30 minutes of PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, with no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet (3 actions, ~25 min total). Facilitator skims exit tickets, shares the three most common "worries" with leadership, and posts one staff-discovered prompt in the staff room or chat. All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Prompt Autopsy" (10 min, below). Leadership states, in writing, the school's interim stance: the three commitments from the session stand until a formal policy exists. PLC leads · Admin
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "Safe or Not?" (10 min, below). Facilitator collects one time-saved story from each team; these become your board/community talking points. PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "One Hour Back" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month: participation, worries resolved and open, stories collected. Decide the date for Kit 2, Prompting Basics. PLC leads · Admin
The quiet goal of the month: every staff member has used AI for at least one real task, safely, and nobody is pretending: the skeptics have been heard, the enthusiasts have guardrails, and the school has a written interim stance.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Ready to run as written. Any team member can lead them; no prep beyond reading the box.

Follow-up #1 · "Prompt Autopsy"  Week 2 · 10 min

Setup (1 min): one volunteer shares a real prompt they tried since the session, plus the AI's response, on a screen or read aloud.

Autopsy (6 min): the team improves it together, one change at a time: What context was missing? What format should we have demanded? What would we push back with? Run the improved prompt live if a device is handy.

Close (3 min): each person writes down one technique they're stealing. The improved prompt goes in the shared staff prompt doc; that doc is the seed of your school's prompt library (Kit 18 grows it).

Follow-up #2 · "Safe or Not?"  Week 3 · 10 min

Warm-up (4 min): read these four aloud; thumbs up (safe) or down (unsafe) as a team:

  1. "Draft a study guide for my unit on the water cycle." (safe)
  2. "Here's my class roster with reading scores. Group them for me." (unsafe: roster + scores)
  3. "Suggest de-escalation phrases for a student who gets frustrated during math." (safe: no identity)
  4. "Summarize this IEP meeting note so I can email it to his caseworker." (unsafe: record contents)

The interesting part (5 min): each person writes one prompt from their own real work that lives near the line, and the team applies the test: could anyone who knows our school figure out who this is? Rewrite the unsafe ones with the 3 moves. Disagreements are the curriculum: surface them, don't settle them by vote; when genuinely unsure, leave it out of the tool.

Close (1 min): any prompt the team couldn't resolve goes to leadership; those edge cases are exactly what a school AI policy exists to answer (Kit 15).

Follow-up #3 · "One Hour Back"  Week 4 · 10 min

Round (6 min): each person, 30 seconds: the single best time-saver AI gave you this month, or the most useful failure you caught. Both count; the failure stories are how the team learns verification.

Harvest (3 min): the two best time-savers get written up in one sentence each and posted to the staff prompt doc. The facilitator collects one story for the board/community update.

Close (1 min): preview Kit 2, Prompting Basics, and confirm its date.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: read the exit-ticket "worry" themes, then respond to the top one in your next staff memo (heard beats perfect)
  • Week 2: put the interim stance in writing: the session's three commitments, signed by the principal, one page
  • Week 3: collect the time-saved stories; they're your answer when a board member or parent asks "what are we doing about AI?"
  • Week 4: schedule Kit 2, and note who emerged as your building's natural AI leads; they're your future facilitators

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
Universal safe practiceStaff can state the One Hard Rule and the 3 de-identification moves unprompted
Real use, not complianceThe staff prompt doc has entries you didn't put there
Honest cultureAt least one shared story is about AI being wrong, and being caught
MomentumKit 2 is on the calendar and nobody had to be convinced

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Follow-ups keep getting bumped from PLC timeShrink, don't skip: even 5 minutes of "Safe or Not?" keeps the habit alive. The sequence matters more than the minutes.
Only the enthusiasts are participatingHave a respected skeptic lead follow-up #3; the "useful failure" round belongs to them, and it reframes participation as scrutiny, not cheerleading.
Someone breaks the ruleCoach privately, not publicly; assume good intent; the sneaky-PII cases are genuinely non-obvious. Re-run "Safe or Not?" with the (anonymized, generalized) case as item 5.
Staff ask questions the team can't answerCollect them verbatim; they're the agenda for Kit 15 (policy workshop) and evidence for the district that governance work is due.
