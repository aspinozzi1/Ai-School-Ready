The talking-points page — zero technology

A strengths / growth / one-ask structure with sentence starters, for the days you want the words ready without opening a laptop.

Opening a strength: "One thing I want to make sure you know is…" · "[Student] has really grown in…" · "Something I appreciate about [student] is…"
Naming a growth area: "An area we're working on together is…" · "Right now, [student] is still building…" · "One thing that would help [student] grow is…"
Making the one ask: "The most useful thing you could do at home is…" · "If we focus on one thing together, I'd suggest…" · "Here's one small thing that would make a real difference…"

Pick one starter from each row, fill in your own specifics, and you have a complete, warm, honest three minutes before the family ever sits down.

The AI Conference Prep page

This is the page nothing else in this niche has: paste de-identified scores and patterns and get back three talking points and one specific ask for home. Never a name, never a roster — a grade level and a pattern only.

Needs a clear ask Steady, keep going Strong, worth naming specifically
Worked example — a 4th grader, 6 out of 10 on a recent multi-digit multiplication quiz, strong on estimation and mental math, slower on the standard algorithm.

Strength: reliable number sense — can estimate an answer before working the problem, which catches most careless mistakes on its own.
Growth area: the standard multiplication algorithm still takes extra time and occasional regrouping errors.
One ask for home: five minutes, three nights a week, of untimed multiplication practice with the standard algorithm — speed can come later, accuracy first.

Copy-paste prompt: three talking points + one ask

"Here is a de-identified snapshot for one student conference: [PASTE GRADE LEVEL, THE SUBJECT OR SKILL, A RECENT SCORE OR PATTERN — NO NAME, NO ROSTER, NO DISABILITY LABEL]. In four short sentences: name one genuine strength, name one growth area in plain language a family will understand, and draft one specific, doable ask for home. Do not use technical jargon, and do not state a grade, diagnosis, or formal evaluation — this is a conversation starter, not a report."

The chatbot drafts language from a pattern. Whether it fits this particular family, and what you actually say in the room, stays your call — every time, for every student.

No-show follow-up note

A short, warm note for the family that missed their slot — keeping the door open instead of letting the miss go unaddressed.

Hi — I missed you at our conference time on . No worries at all — I know schedules get busy. I'd still love to connect about . Would or a quick phone call work better? Let me know what's easiest for you.

Tip: send the no-show note within 48 hours. Waiting until the next conference block reads as "we didn't notice," even when the real reason is a busy week.

De-identification, always

Before any page in this pack goes into a chatbot
Grade level, subject, and a pattern or score only.
Never a name, never a class roster, never a diagnosis or formal label.

This applies to every prompt in this pack, including the AI Conference Prep page. If a sentence you're about to paste would let a stranger identify the student, take out the detail that does it before you paste.

What this kit is not

  • Not a grading or scoring tool. It organizes conferences and helps you find words — it never assigns a grade or a formal rating.
  • Not a compliance document. Follow your school's official conference-scheduling and documentation process; use these pages as your own working tools alongside it.
  • Not legal or clinical advice. It never characterizes a child, never suggests a diagnosis, and never drafts anything that reads as a formal evaluation.
