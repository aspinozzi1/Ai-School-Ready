30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session gave every teacher one rebuilt assignment. The month turns one assignment into a habit. About 30 minutes of existing PLC or staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner

Facilitator reads the exit tickets and pulls two strong sentences from different subjects to send to the whole staff. All staff · Facilitator

The line leadership holds all month
Ask for one thing AI can't hand in. Say where AI stands in one line. And no accusation ever rests on a detector score alone. Any drift from that last line gets a private, kind correction the week it appears.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Could a bot fake it?"  Week 2 · 10 min

Swap (4 min): pairs trade the assignments they rebuilt. Each person reads the partner's added sentence and answers one question honestly: could a chatbot produce this if a student described the class to it?

Push (4 min): any sentence that gets a "maybe" is rewritten one notch more specific on the spot. Naming a date, a person, a piece of local data, or a live moment is usually the fix.

Close (2 min): improved sentences go back on the assignments. The sharpest one is shared with the whole staff as a model.

Follow-up #2 · "The one that won't bend"  Week 3 · 10 min

Setup (2 min): someone brings the assignment they could not anchor. It is usually a skills-practice set, a standardized-format task, or something the department shares word for word.

Work it (6 min): the team tries all three places out loud, in order, and names why the first two fail here. In most of these cases place three is the answer: put two minutes of the thinking in front of you, live, and the rest of the assignment can stay exactly as it is.

Close (2 min): write the team's answer in one sentence and add it to the staff doc. These cases repeat across a building more than anyone expects.

Follow-up #3 · "Assignment number three"  Week 4 · 10 min

Do it (7 min): everyone brings the next assignment on their calendar and runs the four steps live, silently, with the three places on the board. By the third assignment most people finish in five minutes, which is the point of the exercise.

Read two (2 min): two volunteers read their added sentence aloud.

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 6 date.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: ask three teachers what one thing their current assignment asks for that AI can't produce. Instant, concrete answers mean the move landed
  • Week 2: be ready with the parent answer, word for word: "Every assignment says in writing where AI stands, and this one asks for work from our own classroom. And no student here is ever accused based on a detector score."
  • Week 3: collect one concrete artifact for the board: a rebuilt assignment sheet with its added sentence visible. Specifics beat adjectives
  • Week 4: confirm zero detector-only accusations happened. If one did, it's a private coaching conversation this week, not a policy debate next month

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Someone accuses on a detector scoreCoach privately the week you learn of it; re-anchor to the evidence at the next meeting without naming names. This is the one non-negotiable.
Added sentences are vague ("use class discussion")Run follow-up #1 on one of them with the author's blessing. Specific means a date, a person, a number, or a live moment.
A department says it cannot be done in their subjectDo not argue about places one and two. Ask for place three: two minutes of the thinking, live, in front of them. It fits nearly every subject and costs almost nothing.
Someone rebuilds a template instead of an assignmentGently redirect. Anything that looks like a form, a matrix, or a school-wide scale is the failure mode this session was rebuilt to avoid. One sentence, one line, one assignment.
