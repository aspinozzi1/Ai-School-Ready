30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session gave every teacher a labeled assignment, a filled-in AI Box, and a conversation script; the month turns them into a habit, and shows you whether it is worth adopting more widely. About 30 minutes of existing PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet: labeled assignment handed out, Disclosure block taught to one class, two sentences filed. Facilitator reads exit tickets and pulls the best gray-area questions for follow-up #2. All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Box Swap" (10 min, below): colleagues test each other's AI Boxes against the ninth-grader test. PLC leads
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "Gray-Area Calibration" (10 min, below): the team works one real ambiguous case to a shared answer. PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "The Classroom Agreement" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month: labeled assignments visible, disclosure lines appearing on student work, zero detector-only accusations. Schedule Kit 6, AI for Communication. PLC leads · Admin
The line leadership holds all month
Clarity beats policing. Every assignment names its lane, honesty gets a soft landing, and no accusation ever rests on a detector score alone. Any drift from that line gets a private, kind correction the week it appears.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Box Swap"  Week 2 · 10 min

Swap (5 min): pairs trade AI Boxes from real assignments. Each person reads the other's box as a ninth grader would and answers: "do I know exactly what's okay tonight at homework time?" Vague lines get flagged, kindly.

Fix (4 min): authors rewrite one flagged line on the spot, in student words, specific to the assignment.

Close (1 min): improved boxes go back on their assignments; the best one is shared with the whole staff as a model.

Follow-up #2 · "Gray-Area Calibration"  Week 3 · 10 min

Setup (2 min): the facilitator brings one real gray-area case from the exit tickets ("student used AI to translate their own essay"; "AI solved the problem but the student annotated every step"). No student names, ever.

Work it (6 min): the team answers three questions out loud: what lane was the assignment actually in? What would the curiosity-first conversation sound like? What would we change about the box so this case never lands in the gray again?

Close (2 min): write the team's shared answer in one sentence and add it to the staff doc; calibrated judgment is the product.

Follow-up #3 · "The Classroom Agreement"  Week 4 · 10 min

Draft (6 min): each teacher outlines a short classroom AI agreement to co-write with students: when AI is okay here, how we disclose, what happens when something feels off, and the honesty rule. Students follow rules they helped write.

Steal (3 min): pairs trade outlines and take one idea; agreement language repeats across grade levels more than anyone expects.

Close (1 min): pick the week each class writes theirs; confirm the Kit 6 date.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: ask three teachers what lane their current assignment is in; instant answers mean the framework landed
  • Week 2: be ready with the parent answer, word for word: "Every assignment states its AI rules in writing. On that one, here's the box that went home. And no student here is ever accused based on a detector score."
  • Week 3: collect one concrete story for the board: a disclosure line on real student work, or a gray-area case the team calibrated; specifics beat adjectives
  • Week 4: confirm zero detector-only accusations happened; if one did, it's a private coaching conversation this week, not a policy debate next month

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
Lanes are visibleAssignments carry lane labels and boxes; students can quote them
Disclosure is normalTwo-line disclosures appear on lane-2 work, unprompted
Conversations replaced scoresSuspected-use cases start with "walk me through it," and process evidence decides
Honesty is safeStudents who disclose get coaching; nobody hides what they'd have disclosed

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Someone accuses on a detector scoreCoach privately the week you learn of it; re-anchor to the evidence at the next meeting without naming names. This is the one non-negotiable.
Boxes read like legal disclaimersRun Box Swap on one (with the author's blessing) and rewrite it in words a ninth grader says; one good model resets the tone.
Students skip the Disclosure blockCheck the soft landing is real: if a checked box has drawn consequences anywhere, honesty stops. Re-teach the block with the soft-landing rule said out loud.
One department bans everythingThat may be right for their content; ask for lane labels anyway. "All lane 1, and here's why" is clarity too. Unlabeled is the only wrong answer.
