AI & Academic Integrity: Setting Clear Expectations with Students

A 45–60 minute, self-facilitated session in which every teacher writes the AI rules for a real upcoming assignment, in student language, and practices a fair process for suspected misuse. Run by your own coach or teacher-leader; no consultant required.

The stance, and your parent answer
Clarity beats policing. Every assignment states its AI rules in writing, honesty is taught like a skill, and no student is ever accused based on an AI-detector score alone.

Why this matters now

54% of teens use AI for schoolwork (Pew, 2026) while guidance lags far behind adoption (RAND, 2025), so most students are guessing at the rules. Stanford's before-and-after surveys found self-reported cheating flat at 60–70% across ChatGPT's arrival (Lee, Pope et al., 2024): the integrity problem predates the bot, and clarity, not panic, is the fix. Meanwhile the detector route carries real risk: OpenAI shut down its own AI-text detector for low accuracy, and peer-reviewed research found commercial detectors flagged over half of essays by real non-native English speakers as AI-generated (Liang et al., 2023). A false accusation is a family, a lawyer, and a headline. This session replaces scores with process.

What your staff will be able to do afterward

  • Label every assignment with one of three AI lanes: AI-free, AI-assisted, or AI-included
  • Print an AI-expectation box on assignments, in language students can quote back
  • Teach a two-line disclosure norm that makes honest AI use visible and coachable
  • Design assignments where the thinking is visible: drafts, local anchors, live defenses
  • Run a curiosity-first conversation for suspected misuse, built on process evidence
  • State the red line cold: no accusation on a detector score alone, ever
