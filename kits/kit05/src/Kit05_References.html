References

APA style. The note beneath each entry says exactly where the kit uses it, so any claim can be traced to its source.

Doss, C. J., et al. (2025). AI use in schools is quickly increasing but guidance lags behind: Findings from the RAND survey panels (RR-A4180-1). RAND Corporation. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html Used for: in 2024–25, 53% of ELA/math/science teachers and 54% of students used AI for school while guidance lagged adoption; the clarity vacuum this session fills (slide 2; prep guide; admin one-pager).

International Center for Academic Integrity. (2021). The fundamental values of academic integrity (3rd ed.). https://academicintegrity.org/images/pdfs/20019_ICAI-Fundamental-Values_R12.pdf Used for: integrity defined as six values: honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility, and courage (slide 17; handout).

Lee, V. R., Pope, D., Miles, S., & Zárate, R. C. (2024). Cheating in the age of generative AI: A high school survey study of cheating behaviors before and after the release of ChatGPT. Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 7, 100253. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X24000560 Used for: self-reported cheating held at roughly 60–70% of high school students before and after ChatGPT; the rate did not increase, though its forms shift (slide 3; slide 25; prep guide; admin one-pager).

Liang, W., Yuksekgonul, M., Mao, Y., Wu, E., & Zou, J. (2023). GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers. Patterns, 4(7), 100779. https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(23)00130-7 Used for: across seven commercial detectors, more than half of TOEFL essays by real non-native English speakers were misclassified as AI-generated while native samples were almost never flagged (slide 7; handout; prep guide FAQ; admin one-pager).

OpenAI. (2023). New AI classifier for indicating AI-written text (updated July 20, 2023). https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/ Used for: OpenAI discontinued its own AI-text classifier "due to its low rate of accuracy" (26% of AI text caught; 9% of human writing falsely flagged) (slide 7; handout; prep guide FAQ; admin one-pager).

OpenAI. (2026). Terms of use. https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/ Used for: ChatGPT requires users to be 13+, with parent/guardian permission under 18; age rules belong inside classroom AI expectations (slide 6).

Pew Research Center. (2026, February 24). How teens use and view AI. https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/ Used for: 54% of U.S. teens use AI to help with schoolwork (survey of 1,458 teens, Sept–Oct 2025) (slide 2; prep guide; admin one-pager).

Stanford Graduate School of Education. (2023, October 31). What do AI chatbots really mean for students and cheating? https://ed.stanford.edu/news/what-do-ai-chatbots-really-mean-students-and-cheating Used for: students cheat from disengagement, pressure, stress, and not feeling respected far more than from tool access (slide 5; handout; prep guide).

Further reading: five worth your time

  • Lee et al.'s before-and-after cheating study (linked above): the calm-down paper; short, readable, and the best antidote to headline panic.
  • Liang et al.'s detector-bias study (linked above): read it before any detector purchase decision; the TOEFL findings end most debates.
  • ICAI's Fundamental Values (linked above): a one-evening read that reframes integrity as culture to build rather than crime to catch.
  • RAND's adoption-vs-guidance report (linked above): for leaders; shows exactly why so many students have had no rule explained to them.
  • Pew's teen AI survey (linked above): the current numbers on what students actually do, useful verbatim at parent nights.
