# Kit 5 rebuild spec — "Ask for one thing AI can't hand in"

Owner directive, 2026-08-15: the topic (cheating and AI) is strong PD and stays.
The execution failed. Verbatim: *"It didn't flow well and the suggested solutions
and problem solving were more confusing than helpful. The exemplar wasn't succinct,
but instead all over the place."* Rebuild from scratch on this spec.

## What went wrong, so we don't repeat it

The old kit taught SEVEN things in one hour: a three-level lane scale, lanes applied
per assignment part, a one-line label, a teacher-facing AI Box template, a
student-facing Disclosure block template, four assignment upgrades, a conversation
script, and a never list. The running exemplar then used three different lanes inside
one essay (research lane 3, outline lane 2, final draft lane 1), which is why it read
as scattered. Cognitive load, not subject matter, was the problem.

Second problem: the three lanes were a thinner retelling of the **AI Assessment Scale**
(Perkins, Furze, Roe & MacVaugh, 2024), a five-level scale from "No AI" to "AI
Exploration" adopted internationally and translated into 30+ languages, presented in
our kit as if it were ours. Any lane/level language in the rebuild is credited to the
AIAS, and it is never the centerpiece.

## The one move

**Ask for one thing AI can't hand in.**

That is the whole session. A teacher must be able to walk out holding one sentence.
Everything else is either the argument that gets them there or the practice that makes
it real.

The move has exactly three places to look for that "one thing." This is the only
framework in the kit:

1. **From this room** — something that happened here: Tuesday's discussion, the lab we
   ran, the text we annotated together, our own class data. A chatbot was never in the
   room.
2. **From this student** — their own reasoning, choice, or experience, pushed specific
   enough that a generic answer can't fake it.
3. **In front of you** — a piece produced live: a five-minute quick-write, the plan
   before the draft, a two-minute "walk me through it."

One move, three places. Nothing else gets taught as a system.

## The argument, in order (this is the flow the owner said was missing)

Each beat exists to force the next. If a beat can be cut without breaking the chain,
cut it.

1. **You can't tell.** Two short student paragraphs on screen, one written by AI. The
   room votes. Reveal. Most rooms split near chance. Ninety seconds, no statistics yet.
2. **Neither can the software, and it costs somebody.** OpenAI retired its own
   classifier for low accuracy (26% catch rate, 9% false flags). Liang et al. found
   seven detectors flagged more than half of real TOEFL essays by non-native English
   speakers, while native-speaker essays were classified almost perfectly. Detection is
   not a plan, and the students it misfires on are the ones with the least protection.
3. **The relief.** Self-reported cheating did not spike when ChatGPT arrived: across
   the three high schools Lee, Pope et al. surveyed, roughly 60-70% reported at least
   one dishonest behavior both before and after, and prevalence stayed relatively
   stable. State the scope out loud: three schools, self-report, before/after, not
   national causal proof. The point stands anyway — this is not a sudden morality
   collapse, so stop treating it as one.
4. **Then why do they hand in AI work?** Because it is the fastest path to the product
   you asked for. Pope's research points at pressure, disengagement, and workload
   rather than tool access. If the product is all you ask for, AI wins every time, and
   it will keep winning.
5. **So change what you ask for.** The move, and the three places. This is the hinge of
   the hour and it should land at roughly the twenty-minute mark.
6. **Practice, fast.** Three generic prompts on screen. The room adds the one thing to
   each, out loud, sixty seconds apiece. Answers appear on the slide after the room
   tries.
7. **Lab.** Their own real upcoming assignment. See below.
8. **When it still seems off.** The conversation (the owner likes this and it stays):
   open with curiosity, ask them to walk you through it, gather process evidence, land
   it as teaching on a first offense. Plus the hard line: no accusation ever rests on a
   detector score alone.
9. **Close.** Commitments, First 48 Hours, exit ticket.

## The exemplar: ONE assignment, ONE change

Ms. Rivera's 7th grade persuasive essay. It never changes topic, never splits into
parts, and every lab step shows the same document moving one stage further.

- **Before:** "Should our school day start later? Write a five-paragraph persuasive
  essay."
- **What she names it's for:** whether her students can build an argument from evidence
  and answer the strongest objection to it.
- **The one thing she adds (from this room):** "Use at least two claims from our
  Tuesday debate and name who made them. Then explain why the strongest
  counter-argument in the room did not change your mind."
- **Her one-line label:** "AI-assisted. Brainstorming and feedback are fine. The debate
  claims and your response to them have to be yours."
- **Why it works, said plainly:** AI can write a school-start-time essay in four
  seconds. It cannot know what Marcus argued on Tuesday.

The finished product slide shows her assignment sheet as it will print, one sentence
longer than it was. That is the artifact a teacher photographs.

## The lab (protected, >= 15 min)

Four steps, each on one slide, each with Rivera's version of that step shown large:
1. Put your next real assignment on the desk.
2. Name what it is actually for, in one sentence.
3. Add the one thing AI can't hand in. Pick from the three places.
4. Write the one-line label.

They leave holding a rebuilt assignment they will hand out this week.

## What is deliberately CUT (do not reintroduce)

- The three lanes as a taught framework. A one-line label survives; where lane or level
  language appears at all, it credits the AI Assessment Scale.
- The AI Box template (teacher-facing, six fields).
- The AI Disclosure block as a separate student-facing school-wide template. **This
  supersedes the owner's 2026-08-12 two-template ruling**, at the owner's 2026-08-15
  direction to rebuild the solutions; the load those two templates carried is the
  specific thing that made the session confusing. If disclosure is wanted at all it is
  one clause inside the single label line, not a second system.
- The four assignment upgrades (replaced by the one move).
- The never list as its own slide; the one line that matters (never accuse on a score
  alone) lives inside the conversation segment.
- Per-part lane assignments. Never split the exemplar across parts again.
