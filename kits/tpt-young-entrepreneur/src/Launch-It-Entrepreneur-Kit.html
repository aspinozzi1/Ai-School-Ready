Launch It

This is the real thing, sized for a 9-to-12-year-old: a one-page business plan, honest market research, pricing with profit math, pitch practice against a tough (but fair) AI customer, and a ledger that shows whether the numbers actually worked. The entrepreneur does every bit of thinking. The grown-up runs the AI — because that's the law of the land until 13 — and manages anything that touches the internet.

The standing rules
Grown-ups hold the keyboard and run anything online.
Your name, face, school, and street never go into AI tools or onto the internet.

Part 3 · Price it like a pro

The Rule of Three: supplies + your work + a little extra to grow. Then stress-test it.

Do this math before launch day. If the profit looks tiny, raise the price a little or find cheaper supplies — that decision is what business IS.
Grown-up coach prompt · the price test
"A [AGE]-year-old plans to sell [PRODUCT] for [PRICE], with supplies costing [COST] each. Walk through whether that price is fair using the Rule of Three (supplies + labor + growth), compare to what similar things cost, and show the profit math for 5, 10, and 20 sales. Simple table, kid-clear."

Part 4 · The pitch (and the AI customer)

My 30-second pitch:
Name · what it is · why it's great · the price · how to get one. Write it, then say it to a mirror twice.
Grown-up coach prompt · the practice customer
"Play a curious customer at a kids' market. A [AGE]-year-old will pitch you [PRODUCT]. Respond with one compliment, one real objection ('that seems expensive' / 'I could make that myself'), and one question. Stay friendly — firm enough to be good practice, kind enough for a kid. I'll type their replies; keep the conversation going for three rounds."

Why practice against AI? Because the objection you've already heard can't rattle you. Handling "that's too expensive" out loud — politely, with your Rule-of-Three math — is the single most grown-up business skill on this page.

The online-safety contract

If any part of this business touches the internet, we agree:

  • A grown-up creates and runs any listing, page, or post — and handles every message from strangers.
  • My full name, face, school, neighborhood, and schedule stay off the internet, in photos too.
  • We never promise delivery to people we don't know in real life without a grown-up's plan.
  • Money online (payments, apps) is grown-up territory — I see every number, but the accounts are theirs.
  • If anything online feels weird, we stop and talk. The business can wait; this can't.
