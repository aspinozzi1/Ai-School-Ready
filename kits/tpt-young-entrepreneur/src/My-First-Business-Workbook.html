My First Business

A real business, kid-sized: pick an idea, figure out what it costs, set a fair price, make a sign, sell to real people, count real money. Every step has a box for the young entrepreneur — and a coach prompt for the grown-up, because in this family kids do the thinking and grown-ups hold the keyboard.

For the grown-up, before page one
AI chatbots are built for ages 13+ — so the coach prompts are yours to run.
Read results together; your kid picks, rejects, and improves. That's the lesson.

"A [AGE]-year-old is starting a tiny business: [THEIR IDEA]. Suggest five fun, kid-friendly business names and one tip to make the idea even better. Playful, not corporate."

Step 3 · The Rule of Three (how real sellers price)

A fair price covers three things. Fill in each box, add them up — that's your price!

what one item costs to make

My price: $______   ·   If I sell one, I keep: $______
Price minus supplies = your profit. Say that word to a grown-up tonight. You're officially in business.

Step 4 · Tell the world (kid-safe marketing)

Design your sign here first — big letters, your price, one reason to buy:
My selling sentence (what I'll say to customers):
Grown-up coach prompt
"A [AGE]-year-old is making a sign for their [BUSINESS]. Give three short, catchy slogan ideas a kid could letter by hand, and two friendly things to say when a customer walks up. No pressure-selling — neighborly and fun."

Safety, stated once, meant always: selling happens to people we know — family, neighbors, co-op, the block party. If anything goes online, a grown-up posts it and handles every message, and your name, face, school, and street stay off the internet. That's not a maybe; that's the deal.
