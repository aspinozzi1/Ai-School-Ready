AI for Workload: Winning Back an Hour a Week

A 45–60 minute, self-facilitated session: every teacher audits their real week, templates their two worst repeating tasks, and writes a protected list of the work that stays human. Run by your own coach; no consultant required.

The stance, and your staff answer
Hand the repetitive to the tool; keep the relational on purpose. Recovered hours go to teaching, planning, or leaving on time, never to new busywork.

Why this matters now

In Gallup's February 2022 workforce survey, K-12 reported the highest burnout of the industries measured: 44% of workers and 52% of teachers "always or very often" burned out (Gallup, 2022), inside a ~54-hour week where less than half is teaching (Merrimack/EdWeek, 2022). Teachers using AI weekly report ~5.9 hours a week back, about six weeks a year (Gallup, 2025). This session locates each teacher's share of those hours, and writes down what the hours are for.

What your staff will be able to do afterward

  • Audit a week with the two-question sort: needs my kids? does it repeat?
  • Template recurring tasks: build once with placeholders, test twice, reuse forever
  • Batch same-kind tasks and share templates in the staff library
  • Keep a written protected list: the relational work no tool touches
  • Hold the review rule so "AI drafts it" never drifts into "AI decides it"
  • Keep the privacy rule: no student information in public tools, at any workload
