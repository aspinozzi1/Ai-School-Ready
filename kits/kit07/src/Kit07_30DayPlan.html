30-Day Implementation & Follow-Up Plan

The session located the hours; the month makes them permanent. About 30 minutes of existing PLC/staff-meeting time across four weeks, no new meetings, and one leadership commitment that matters more than all of it.

Week by week

WeekWhat happensOwner
Week 1 Everyone completes the First 48 Hours sheet: both templates run for real, the first recovered minutes spent on purpose. Facilitator reads exit tickets and pulls the most-requested "still want help automating" tasks for follow-up #1. All staff · Facilitator
Week 2 PLC follow-up #1, "Template Clinic" (10 min, below): fix the wobbles, template one popular request together. PLC leads
Week 3 PLC follow-up #2, "Library Sprint" (10 min, below): the Workload section gets every working template in the building. PLC leads
Week 4 PLC follow-up #3, "The Reckoning" (10 min, below). Leadership reviews the month: templates in use, hours named, nothing relational delegated. Schedule Kit 8, the Track A capstone. PLC leads · Admin
The line leadership holds all month
Recovered time is not a vacuum to fill. No new initiatives get launched into the hours this kit returns. Say that out loud, once, in public; it's the single highest-trust move available to a leader this month.

The three 10-minute PLC follow-ups

Follow-up #1 · "Template Clinic"  Week 2 · 10 min

Round (4 min): each person: are your two templates alive? One wobble each, named.

Fix (5 min): the team repairs the most common wobble together (it's almost always a missing context line), then templates one task from the exit-ticket request list, live.

Close (1 min): repaired templates back to the staff doc.

Follow-up #2 · "Library Sprint"  Week 3 · 10 min

Sprint (7 min): everyone posts every working template they have to the Workload section: name, one line on what it's for. Duplicates get merged; the best version wins.

Steal (2 min): everyone leaves with one template they didn't write and a real task to run it on this week.

Close (1 min): count the library. Thirty-plus templates is a different school than it was a month ago.

Follow-up #3 · "The Reckoning"  Week 4 · 10 min

Round (6 min): the honest question, each person: where did the recovered time actually go? Celebrate the human answers (planning, kids, leaving on time); name the refills (new busywork) without judgment.

Protect (3 min): one line each from the protected lists, read aloud. This is the faculty saying what teaching is for; let it land.

Close (1 min): confirm the Kit 8 date: the capstone that turns all of it into culture.

Leadership checklist for the month

  • Week 1: say the no-refill line in public: recovered hours are not a slot for new initiatives
  • Week 2: ask three teachers which task they deleted, not whether they liked the PD
  • Week 3: skim the Workload library; thank three contributors by name
  • Week 4: collect one story for the board: a template the whole building now uses, or an honest answer about where the hours went

What "working" looks like at day 30

SignalHow you'll know
Templates are aliveSub plans, exit tickets, newsletters come from the library; from-scratch is the exception
The library compoundsThe Workload section grows weekly; people steal freely and say so
The hours stay wonTeachers can name where their time went; the refill didn't happen
The lines holdEverything reviewed; nothing relational delegated; protected lists quoted unprompted

If the month goes sideways

SymptomFix
Templates go stale after week oneThe blocker is friction: templates not saved where work happens. Pin the Workload section; make it the first tab, not a buried doc.
Output ships unreviewedThat's the drift, and it's the kit's one non-negotiable. Private, kind correction the week it appears; re-anchor at the next meeting without names.
The recovered hours get refilledIf leadership filled them: own it and roll it back publicly; trust rides on this. If the pile refilled them: rerun the audit; the two questions still work.
Someone templates the relationalThe protected list exists for exactly this moment. Coach privately: if it requires knowing the kids, it isn't draftable, no matter how tired the week was.
