The 6-tab binder system

Six sections cover every question a sub actually asks. Build once; refresh, don't rebuild.

Tab 1
Daily Schedule
Bell times, where the sub goes each period, duty coverage, the day's special events.
Tab 2
Rosters & Seating
Printed class lists and seating charts — names live here, on paper, never in the AI.
Tab 3
Emergency Procedures
Fire, lockdown, evacuation map, and the office/nurse extensions a sub will need fast.
Tab 4
Ready-to-Grab Activity
One no-copier, no-login backup activity per prep, for the day everything else fails.
Tab 5
Class Routines & Culture
Entering, dismissal, signals, and a one-page "how this room runs."
Tab 6
Today's Lesson
The one thing that changes every time — filled in fresh, the morning you need it.

The master day-drafter prompt

A full sub day, drafted for a stranger

"You are an experienced [GRADE/SUBJECT] teacher writing plans for a substitute who has never met these students and may not know the subject. Today's classes: [LIST PERIODS AND LENGTHS]. We are currently studying [TOPIC/UNIT]. Write a complete sub plan: for each period, a 5-minute bell-ringer, one main activity with numbered student-facing directions, an early-finisher task, and how to collect work. Nothing that needs a copier, a login, or subject expertise. Tone: calm and specific. End with a three-line note the sub can leave me about how it went."

Print the result straight into Tab 6 — it's the only tab that changes every time you're out.

Five prompts, one per tab

Tab 1 · Turn your schedule into a sub-readable page

"Format this bell schedule into a clean, large-print one-pager for a substitute: [PASTE SCHEDULE]. Add a one-line note under each period about where the sub should be and what's different that day, if anything."

Tab 3 · The emergency quick-reference card

"Turn this into a large-print emergency quick-reference card for a substitute: [PASTE YOUR FIRE/LOCKDOWN PROCEDURES AND KEY PHONE NUMBERS]. One line per procedure, boldest text for what to do first."

Tab 4 · The no-tech backup activity

"Give me one paper-and-pencil backup activity per prep for [GRADE/SUBJECTS], each tied loosely to the general curriculum, zero technology, zero copier — something a sub can run cold with directions printed right on the page."

Tab 5 · The "how this room runs" page

"Write a short 'how this class runs' page for a substitute: routines for entering, bathroom, and turning in work: [DESCRIBE YOUR ROUTINES]. Do not reference any individual student — I keep those notes separately."

Tab 6 · The review-day fallback

"Instead of new content, make this a review day on [LAST TOPIC]: a 15-question mixed review students can do independently, an answer key on a separate page, and one partner activity that stays quiet. Use when I'm too sick to describe today's plan."

The quarterly refresh checklist

Fifteen minutes, four times a year — the binder stays current instead of going stale.

Swap the roster and seating chart for every class you teach.
5 min
Update the schedule page if bell times, duties, or specials changed.
3 min
Re-check emergency numbers against the current staff directory.
2 min
Refresh the backup activity if your unit or grade level moved on.
3 min
Ask a colleague to glance through it — a second set of eyes catches what you'd miss.
2 min

Print & assembly

  • Print the cover page, six tab dividers, and every prompt output on card stock if you have it — it holds up to a binder better.
  • Assemble in a standard three-ring binder, cover page in the front pocket, dividers in tab order, Tab 6 loaded fresh each time you're planning to be out.
  • Store it somewhere your building sub coordinator or front office knows to find it — the binder only works if someone can grab it at 5:30 a.m.
