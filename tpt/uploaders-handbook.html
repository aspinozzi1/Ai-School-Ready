The Uploader's Handbook

Everything arrives finished. Your job is the last mile: open a drop, work down one text file, press publish, next drop. This page is the whole ritual — and page two is what to do when something looks off, because your eyes are the final quality gate this whole factory answers to.

What arrives, and when

  • Every Monday morning a phone notification and an email say the week's batch is ready. The files are attached in the Claude session the notification links to — about 10–11 products: classroom frees and paid items, plus the homeschool / young-entrepreneur slice.
  • Each product = one drop zip, numbered in upload order: drop-24-…zip, drop-25-…zip, and so on.
  • One pins zip rides along each week — Pinterest images plus their own copy-paste sheet.
  • Everything is also archived in the GitHub repo under UPLOAD/drops/, forever, in case a download is lost.

The upload ritual (per drop, about 4 minutes)

1
Unzip the drop and open LISTING.txt. It lists every field in the exact order TPT's "Upload New Product" page shows them, top to bottom. You never have to decide anything — just copy, paste, and pick.
2
Work down the file. Every other file in the zip is named for the form slot it belongs to (the table below). Paste the title, upload the files where named, paste the description exactly as written, set the price and license price it states.
3
Pick the dropdown categories — grades, subjects, tags — from the list in LISTING.txt. TPT's labels sometimes differ slightly; choose the closest one.
4
Publish. Next drop. After the drops, open the pins zip and its PINS.txt — same idea, but for Pinterest's Create Pin form. You can upload all the pin images at once and work through the drafts list.

You are the last quality gate

Before a drop reaches you it has passed automated checks — page breaks, file integrity, honest page counts, pricing rules, preview freshness. But the line on every product says "built and audited by two certified teachers," and you're one of them. That line is a promise, and it's kept at your desk.

The 60-second teacher read, per product:

  • Flip the product PDF once. Does it look like something you'd be glad to download? Any page that makes you wince — cramped, cut off, half-empty, off-tone?
  • Spot-check one claim. If the cover says "10 prompts" or "5 pages," count once.
  • Read one paragraph out loud. Does it sound like a real teacher wrote it for a real teacher? That voice is the brand.
  • Trust your classroom instincts over the checklist. If something feels pedagogically off or unsafe — especially anything involving students or kids and AI — that overrules everything.

If something looks wrong

  • Don't fix it by hand, and don't upload it. Skip that drop, publish the rest.
  • Say what you saw in the Claude session (or tell Adam to): a screenshot and one sentence — "the letter on page 3 is cut off" — is plenty. The fix gets made at the source, every derived file (preview, thumbnails, pins) gets rebuilt, and a corrected drop comes back. Hand-fixed uploads drift out of sync with the factory; reported ones make the factory smarter — several of the automated gates exist because someone spotted something once.

House rules worth knowing (the "why" behind odd details)

  • Additional licenses are about half the product price ($4 on $8, $6 on $12, $10 on $24) — TPT rejects a license priced at or above the product, and half-price is what makes whole-staff purchases happen.
  • Titles are under 80 characters because TPT cuts longer ones mid-word.
  • We never promise future content in a listing — no "part 2 coming soon."
  • Nothing links outside TPT. No website, no email list — the store is the whole operation.
  • Kid-facing products (ages 4–12) never have children using chatbots — the grown-up holds the keyboard, always. If you ever see a page that breaks this, that's the most important catch you can make.
  • On Pinterest, the "Mark as AI-Modified" toggle goes ON. It's the honest disclosure, and honesty is the brand's whole trust strategy.
That's the entire job: open, paste, publish, glance with teacher eyes, flag anything weird. The research, writing, design, pricing, and packaging all happen upstream — and every Monday it starts again.
