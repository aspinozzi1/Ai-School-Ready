## Before you run them: why I am arguing against game 3 being the quiz show

I recommended the escape room two weeks ago and I would again. **I do not think
the quiz show is the same kind of opportunity**, and you should have the reason
before you spend four pulls.

**1 — We would be competing with ourselves.** The Long Road is already the
one-projector, two-teams, no-student-devices review game. A quiz-show board is
the same lesson, the same room and the same buyer. **The second product does not
double the sales; it splits them.**

**2 — The free alternatives here are genuinely good**, which was not true of the
escape room. Factile builds quiz-show games free, students buzz in from their own
phones with a PIN and no student accounts, and it says it is used in 5,700-plus
districts. JeopardyLabs hands over pre-made games by the million. The honest
answer to "why not use the free one" is thinner than it was for a locked room.

**3 — The phrase teachers search is one we cannot print.** Using "Jeopardy®" in a
product name is trademark infringement; the game-show *format* is free to build
on, and sellers rename to trivia or knowledge or brain-game wording. So row 1 can
tell us the demand is there and we still cannot put the word in the title. **That
is the opposite of the escape room, where the search phrase and the legal title
were the same words.**

**What would change my mind:** row 2 or row 3 coming back with real volume and a
Promising or Strong badge. Then the demand exists under a name we can actually
use, and the Long Road overlap is worth eating.

### What I would build instead, in order of preference

- **The name picker and group maker, $9** (row 4). Smallest product in the line,
  fastest to something useful, and the research already says it is what teachers
  reach for. The honest competition page is easy here because the free pickers
  forget everything the moment you close the tab, and ours remembers.
- **A game 3 the free tools do not serve** — a deduction or case-file game where
  clues eliminate suspects and the teacher supplies the clue set from a setup
  screen. Different world, different mechanic, no overlap with the Long Road.
  **Demand entirely unmeasured**, which is why it is second and not first.

## Two things from today that change what Sunday has to do

**1 · Every product is now one zip, and that is fixed at the source.** You found
that TPT's Downloadable File box takes a single file. Ten listings were shipping
a PDF plus a loose app file with instructions to upload both, which the form does
not allow. The packager now bundles them into one zip automatically. **Two of
those ten are already live — Pitch to Prototype and the Class Roster — so every
buyer so far got the guide without the app.** Those two listings need their
download replaced, and it may be worth a note to anyone who already bought.

**2 · The lesson has to be readable off the cover.** You said drop 60 was hard to
understand. It was: The Class Store carried five competing claims to being the
lesson and the title described the activity instead of what it teaches. That is
now fixed for 60, and **I am treating it as a standing requirement rather than a
one-off** — every build from here states one sentence on the cover and points
every day of it at that sentence. It is the same complaint as 09-23 in a
different costume, and the common cause is building before deciding what the one
thing is.

If you want it, the same audit applies to Pitch to Prototype and the two game
