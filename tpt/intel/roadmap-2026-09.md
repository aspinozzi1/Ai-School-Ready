## The governing decision: concentrate, don't expand

Three drop days is ~14 products a week. The risk is **catalog sprawl** —
forty-plus listings spread thin, none deep enough to rank, in a
marketplace that rewards depth and cross-selling. The fix is not fewer
drop days; it is narrowing what each day does:

- **Sunday** builds the **data-tracking family** until it is deep.
- **Wednesday** builds Young Entrepreneurs and Deaf Education.
- **Friday** builds one binder, unit by unit, until it bundles.

**Rule for every factory: prefer a second product in a family we already
hold over a first product in a new one**, unless the new one outranks
everything open on win score.

## Why UFLI waits

"ufli" is 17,926 searches at 20K–40K, Strong — win score ~597, the
largest opening in the dataset. It waits anyway, for two reasons:

1. **Ranking follows sales velocity and reviews.** Entering the most
   contested phrase we have found while the store has no sales history
   means fighting established sellers on their strongest ground with
   none of our own. The same build in six weeks performs materially
   better. UFLI adoption is growing, so the opportunity is not
   perishable.
2. **It is a named program and a trademark.** If we do it, we work from
   the published scope and sequence, label it plainly a supplement, and
   state non-affiliation on the listing. We never imply endorsement. The
   trademark question is a legal one and is the owner's call to take
   advice on, not ours to assume away.

## The honest economics

A $5 unit nets roughly $2–3 after TPT's cut on a basic seller account.
The revenue difference between chasing UFLI now versus in October is
plausibly tens of dollars. The difference between **having reviews and
not having reviews** compounds across every listing in the store,
permanently. That asymmetry is the whole argument.

Where the money actually is, in order:
1. **Bundles** — the $18 CVC bundle, not the $5 units.
2. **$8–12 paid items in families where our frees already funnel.**
3. **Review velocity**, which is why the frees matter more than they look.

## The bottleneck that is not a build

The highest-leverage action available is not authoring anything: it is
**getting the first reviews on the free products already live**. Ranking
follows reviews. Until that moves, every keyword decision is theoretical.
