## My recommendation for Sunday, for you to overrule

**Build the escape room — game 2 in the series you said you want to own.**

### Four reasons, and one of them is a risk

1. **You named the series.** "Vibe Code Your Own Game" is the thing you said you
   should own on TPT, and the momentum belongs there rather than in a seating
   chart.
2. **Escape rooms are a live, crowded, real market** — which is the opposite of
   the `vibe coding` situation. Teachers search for these, buy these and review
   these today. For once the demand is not a bet.
3. **The differentiator is sharp and true.** Most classroom escape rooms sold
   today are a Google Form or a slide deck with a code to type. Ours is **actual
   software**: real locked doors, puzzles the teacher changes from a setup
   screen, working offline, no accounts, and a file they own. That is a real
   difference and it is easy to show on a cover.
4. **It answers a design worry I raised and never resolved.** I flagged that if
   every game in the series opens on a dusk landscape they will look the same by
   build three. **An escape room is interiors, doors and locks** — a different
   visual world by construction rather than by decoration.

**The risk, stated plainly: escape room is probably the most crowded shelf this
store would ever enter.** Rows 1 to 3 are there to find out how bad, and whether
the qualified phrase is reachable. If all three come back brutal, build the
seating chart instead — it is unmeasured, but unmeasured beats measured-and-full.

### What it would be, under the new product shape

The tool, plus six to ten pages, at **$12–14**. Opens and works with a sample
room already in it. The teacher's own puzzles go in from a **setup screen**, and
the same screen carries an **AI prompt** that drafts a whole room from their unit
in about thirty seconds. No code edited, ever. Same shape as The Long Road and
The Class Store, which is now the house pattern.
