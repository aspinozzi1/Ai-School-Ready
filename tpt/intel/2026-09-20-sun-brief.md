## (a) THE BUILD — a behavior point card that keeps its history

**Build 3 in the Lane A series. Title it on the tracker, not the card.**

### Why this one next, and why not the two ahead of it in the roadmap

`vibe-coding-line.md` orders the next builds **#3 seating chart · #4 hall
pass · #5 behavior points tracker.** I am moving #5 to Sunday. Both
reasons were sent to the owner in `lookups-2026-W39-sunday.md` on Friday
and neither has been overruled; **if the owner has since said otherwise,
that instruction wins over this file.**

1. **It is the only Lane A item with validated artifact phrases.** Every
   other item on the roadmap has never been measured on TPT.
2. **The hall pass build should probably not happen at all.** Digital
   hall passes became a student-surveillance story in 2026 —
   Philadelphia district-wide for 2026-27, NYC systems timing bathroom
   trips. A log of which named child left the room and for how long is
   tier 3 under our own rule, and teaching a teacher to build one
   contradicts the posture of every other product in this catalog.
3. **The seating chart faces seven free generators on the open web**,
   several AI-powered. Buildable, but it needs an answer to "why not use
   the free one" baked in, and that is a harder product than this one.

### Why it is not a duplicate of the dashboard we shipped on 09-16

The shipped dashboard has a Points tab. It holds **a running total per
student and nothing else** — no dates, no history, no way to answer "is
this better than three weeks ago". That is a tally, not a tracker.

**The lesson that makes build 3 a real step up: build 2 taught the app to
remember a *value*. Build 3 teaches it to remember a *record over time*,
which means the teacher has to decide on a data shape before they prompt.**
That is the first genuinely architectural idea in the series and it is
the one that separates somebody who can vibe-code a toy from somebody who
can vibe-code a tool.

Say this in the pack explicitly. It is the sentence the product is for.

### The real-world artifact this is, by name

**Check-In / Check-Out (CICO)** — per the literature, the most frequently
used Tier 2 behavior intervention in US schools. A student checks in with
an adult in the morning, carries a **daily point card** scored through the
day against three to five expectations, checks out in the afternoon, and
a copy goes home. It runs **six to eight weeks** and is reviewed on data.

The factory does **not** need to teach CICO, and must not present the pack
as a behavior intervention program — **we record and summarize, we never
prescribe the team's intervention.** But the tool should fit the shape
teachers already use, because that is why they will want it.

### The ten tasks, each ending somewhere definite

| # | Task | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The shell and the roster — **student numbers or initials, not names** | A page that lists the class and survives nothing yet |
| 2 | Name the expectations — three to five, in the teacher's own words | The teacher's own expectations on screen, editable |
| 3 | The day grid — time blocks down, expectations across, tap to score | A day that can be filled in, in under a minute |
| 4 | **Make it remember** — and here, remember a *shape* | Close the tab, reopen it, today's scores are still there |
| 5 | The day roll-up — points earned over points possible, as a percentage | One number per child per day, against a goal the teacher sets |
| 6 | The week view — five days side by side | A trend a human can see without doing arithmetic |
| 7 | **Export and the weekly backup habit** | A file on the teacher's own drive, and a reason they will actually do it |
| 8 | The printable day card | One page per child that can go home in a backpack |
| 9 | The de-identified summary | Text with every identifier replaced, ready to paste into AI |
| 10 | Colors, accessibility, and the shared-computer check | A tool that works on the classroom machine, for the person who owns it |

**Stop-anywhere still applies.** A teacher who finishes tasks 1–5 has a
working daily point card. Say so, so they start.

## (d) DESIGN AND SAFETY — specific to this tool

### What the app must remember

Not a number. **A list of dated records**, each one: which student, which
day, which time block, which expectation, what score. Everything else —
the day percentage, the week view, the trend — is computed from that list.

**Teach the shape before the prompt.** If the teacher lets the AI decide
this, they get a tally that cannot answer a question about last Tuesday,
and by the time they notice they have three weeks of unusable data.

### Where the data lives, and the three-tier page for *this* build

The tier is decided by **where the data lives and who can reach it**, not
by whether names appear. For a behavior record that is stricter than
usual, because **a dated behavior record about an identified child is more
sensitive than a class roster.**

- **Tier 1 — build freely.** The card design, the expectations, the goal
  percentage, the blank printable. All teacher-authored.
- **Tier 2 — the app's home.** Daily scores kept **by student number or
  initials** on the teacher's own device, in that browser, transmitted
  nowhere. The app defaults to student numbers, and that default is
  taught as the right choice rather than offered as a setting.
- **Tier 3 — stop and ask the district.** The moment those records are
  synced, emailed, dropped in a shared drive, pasted into a chatbot, or
  opened on a computer somebody else signs into — they have left the
  teacher's device. Also: this is **the teacher's own working record, not
  the school's record of behavior.** The district's system is the record
  of record, and the pack says so plainly.

### The failure modes a teacher will actually hit with *this* build

1. **A shared classroom computer.** Browser storage is per browser
   profile, not per person. If a substitute, a co-teacher or a student
   uses that machine on the same profile, they can open the tracker. This
   is the single biggest hazard specific to this tool and it does not
   apply to a name picker. Task 10 makes the teacher check it.
2. **Clearing browsing data wipes six weeks of intervention.** The whole
   value is the trend line. **This is why task 7 exists and why it is not
   optional** — a weekly export is the habit, and the pack should say what
   day to do it.
3. **The AI builds a tally instead of a record** — the default it reaches
   for. The prompt has to say "keep every entry with its date", and the
   pack shows the teacher how to tell which one they got.
4. **Scoring every block for every child is too slow** and the tool gets
   abandoned in week two. One child, three to five blocks, taps not
   typing. Say so.
5. **Percentages computed off a changing denominator.** A day with two
   blocks scored is not comparable to a day with six. The roll-up must
   show earned over possible, not a bare percentage.
6. **Printing a page meant for a screen** produces something unusable in
   a backpack. Task 8 is its own task for that reason.

### The app must do, feature by feature, so the factory can test it

- Add students by number or initials; no full-name field anywhere.
- Set three to five expectations in the teacher's own words; editable later.
- Score a day: time blocks × expectations, tap to cycle the score.
- Day roll-up: earned / possible and a percentage, against a teacher-set goal.
- Week view: five days per child, with the goal line visible.
- Everything survives a tab close and reopen.
- Export to a file, and a visible prompt when it has been a week.
- Print a single child's day card on one page.
- De-identified summary text with identifiers replaced.
- Keyboard reachable, readable contrast, works if storage is unavailable.

**Test all of it in a browser before shipping and report what was
tested** — including a reload between two scoring sessions, which is the
one that matters.
