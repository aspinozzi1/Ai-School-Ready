## Read this first: I am recommending a change to the Lane A roadmap

`vibe-coding-line.md` puts the next three Lane A builds in this order —
**#3 seating chart builder · #4 hall pass and sign-out log · #5 behavior
points tracker.** This week's research says that order is wrong, on two
separate grounds.

`free behavior tracker` at 2,644 is the **second-best score in the entire

Both numbers are 13 days old. Inside the three-week window, but rows 1
and 2 below re-pull them anyway, because this decides a flagship.

### 2. Drop or reframe the hall pass build — evidence, not squeamishness

Digital hall passes became a **student-surveillance controversy** during
2026. Philadelphia is rolling a district system to every school for
2026-27; NYC's system tracks how long a child spends in the bathroom and
who else asked to go; the reporting is explicitly about monitoring and
the objections to it.

Two problems, and the second is the one that decides it:

- **Data tier.** A log of which named child left the room, when, and for
  how long is traceable to a child. That is tier 3 under our own rule —
  the tier where we stop and the district decides.
- **What we would be teaching.** "Build your own system for tracking
  where children go" is the opposite of the posture every other product
  in this catalog takes. It is not a thing I think this store should
  teach a teacher to build, whatever it measures.

There is a good product hiding next to it: a **sign-out sheet the class
owns** — a printable log the teacher keeps on their own device for their
own room, with no administrator dashboard and no timing of anybody. If
you want that build, it is a different product with a different title,
and rows 11 and 12 size it. **Your call, not mine — but I did not want
this sitting unsaid in a roadmap table.**

### 3. The seating chart build has a competitor problem worth knowing before we price it

There are at least seven free seating-chart generators on the open web
right now, several AI-powered, several handling behavior separation and
accessibility constraints. That is genuine demand evidence. It is also a
real objection a buyer will raise, and the answer has to be in the
product: **it remembers your layout, it prints, it is yours, and no class
roster goes onto a stranger's server.** Rows 4 and 5 tell us whether TPT
buyers search for it at all, which is a separate question from whether
the web has tools for it.
