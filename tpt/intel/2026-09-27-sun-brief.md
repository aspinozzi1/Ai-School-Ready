### Why an escape room, and why now

1. **It is a real market rather than a bet.** Unlike `vibe coding`, teachers
   search for, buy and review escape rooms today.
2. **The differentiator is true and shows on a cover.** Most classroom escape
   rooms sold on TPT are a Google Form or a slide deck with a code to type.
   **Ours is actual software** — real locks, clues the teacher changes from a
   setup screen, offline, no accounts, a file they own.
3. **It fixes a design problem I raised on 09-22 and never resolved.** If every
   game in the series opens on a dusk landscape they look the same by build
   three. **An escape room is an interior** — a different visual world by
   construction, not by decoration.

**Lane C note, one line:** this is the strongest candidate yet for Kit 3's lab
file after the PD ships, because a room full of teachers each leaving with an
escape room for their own subject is the session people talk about.

### The shape, and how it meets the standard structurally rather than by bolting on

`tpt/intel/GAME-DESIGN-STANDARD.md` is binding and its gate is a checklist. The
shape below is chosen so each line is met by the design, not by a feature:

| Standard's line | How the shape delivers it |
|---|---|
| A real decision every turn that is not "pick the right answer" | **The room has four or five locks visible at once and the player chooses which to attack.** Each lock is a different idea at a different difficulty, and the choice comes before any question |
| Two students, different runs | Which locks they attack, in which order, and which jam — no two rooms open the same way |
| Difficulty follows the player | **The player picks it by picking the lock.** The stiff lock asks a level 3 and opens two doors; the simple one asks a level 1 and opens a drawer |
| Missed ideas return | **A wrong answer jams that lock.** It stays on screen, visibly jammed, and can be tried again later — the idea is in the room until it is fixed |
| Fixing a miss is the loudest moment | **Clearing a jammed lock is the only full-screen celebration in the game**, and it opens something a working lock does not |
| No public failure, no losing | **There is no timer and no fail state.** A jammed lock costs a route, not the game. Every room opens eventually; the score is *locks opened without jamming* and *jams cleared* |
| Interleaving | The draw never offers two locks on the same idea side by side |
| Growth, not points | The tally is **ideas cracked**, and it only goes up |

**Say the jam rule out loud in the pack.** It is the whole design: a wrong
answer does not end anything, it puts the idea back in the room where everyone
can see it, and going back for it is the best thing that happens.

### What the tool does out of the box

Opens by double-click. One HTML file, no install, no accounts, works offline.
**A sample room is already in it** — the same water-cycle set the series uses —
so it plays before the teacher reads a word.

### What the setup screen must collect

Everything through a form. **No teacher edits code, ever.**

- **The room's name** and **what is behind the final door** (one line of flavor:
  "the supply closet," "the roof," "Friday").
- **The clues**, one per lock: the question, four answers with the right one
  marked, the **idea** it tests, the **level** 1–3, and the **why** line shown
  after a wrong answer.
- **Eight to fourteen clues across three or four ideas** is a room. The screen
  warns below eight and when only one idea is used.

### The AI drafting prompt, and the format it returns

On the setup screen, copy-and-paste, filled with whatever the teacher typed as
the subject. Same pipe format as the other tools, because the parser is the
same and it already reports the lines it skips:

`question | correct answer | wrong | wrong | wrong | idea | level | why`

Instruction to the factory: keep the wording that tells the AI to use only
three or four ideas across the set, to spread levels roughly evenly, and to
write the *why* line for a student who just got it wrong. **And keep "read them
before you play them" on the screen** — that line is now in every Lane A tool.

### The three change-it prompts — three, not twelve

1. **Change the room.** "Change the setting from a storeroom to a ship's cabin.
   Keep every rule exactly as it is — the locks, the jamming, the celebration.
   Change only the words, the colors and the shapes drawn. Whole file back."
2. **Change the room's size.** "Make a room have six locks instead of the
   current number, and tell me which line I change to do that myself next time."
3. **Read it from the back.** "Increase every font size by about a quarter and
   raise the contrast on the lock buttons, keeping the layout intact at 1280 by
   720 on a projector. Whole file back."

Plus the three standing rules: one change per prompt, always say *give me the
whole file back*, always save under a new name.

### The art — interiors, and the standard's Look rules still apply

**No pixel art.** A wall as a vertical gradient, a floor plane, furniture and
doors as **silhouettes with one light source** — a lamp or a window — and a rim
light on the edges facing it. Locks as simple geometric shapes that read at a
distance: a padlock is a rectangle and an arc. One palette, sixteen colors or
fewer. **Every shape must pass the black-silhouette test** and be readable from
the back of a room at 1x.

Reuse the drawing approach from `kits/tpt-game-longroad/src/long-road.html` —
gradients, paths, rim light — not its landscape functions.

## (d) DESIGN AND SAFETY — specific to this tool

### The three-tier page, sized to a game that knows nothing

- **Tier 1 — build freely.** The room's name, the clues, the answers, the why
  lines, the art. All teacher-authored, none of it about a child.
- **Tier 2 — the teacher's own device.** The saved room file, and the best-run
  record if there is one — **ideas cracked and jams cleared, never a name.**
- **Tier 3 — stop.** Any of it attached to a named student, or pasted into a
  chatbot alongside a child's work. Nothing in this tool requires that, which is
  the point.

**The named-leaderboard request is refused in the product and the reason given**,
the same as the other games: public individual ranking does the most damage to
the students this was built for.

### The failure modes specific to *this* tool

1. **A room with one idea in it.** The commonest setup error, and it breaks
   interleaving and the jam mechanic at once. The setup screen warns.
2. **Every lock at level 1**, so choosing a lock is not a real decision. Warn
   when no level 3 exists.
3. **Too few clues.** Below eight the room opens in ninety seconds. The screen
   says so rather than letting it happen in front of a class.
4. **The AI writes four plausible answers where two are right.** A distractor
   has to be *wrong*, and in an escape room a disputed answer stops the whole
   room. Tell the teacher to read for this specifically.
5. **A jammed lock with no other route**, leaving the player stuck. **The room
   must always have at least one openable lock** — if every remaining lock is
   jammed, jammed ones become retryable immediately. Test this path.
6. **Projector contrast.** An interior is darker than a landscape. Check the
   lock buttons at 1280 by 720 before shipping.

### What the tool must do, feature by feature, so the factory can test it

- Opens by double-click, offline, no accounts, sample room already loaded.
- Setup screen: room name, final-door line, and a row per clue (question, four
  answers, correct marked, idea, level, why). Warnings for under eight clues,
  a single idea, and no level 3.
- AI prompt on that screen with a copy button, and a paste box whose parser
  **names the lines it skipped and why**.
- Play: four or five locks visible, choose one, answer, **right opens it and
  wrong jams it** with the why line held longer.
- **A jammed lock is retryable, and clearing one is the only full-screen
  celebration.**
- Never two locks on the same idea side by side.
- Final door opens when enough locks are open; the end screen reports **ideas
  cracked and jams cleared**, no names.
- **Save the room as a file** that reopens with the teacher's clues in it.
- Keyboard reachable, readable contrast.

**Test in a browser and report what was returned**: play to the final door,
jam a lock on purpose and clear it, drive the all-locks-jammed path from
failure 5, run the paste parser with one bad line, save and reopen the file,
and confirm zero console errors on every path.
