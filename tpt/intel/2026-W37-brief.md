**Contents (~14 pages):**

2. Goal-at-a-glance page (one goal per sheet: goal text, baseline, target, criterion)
3. Weekly data sheet — 12 weeks, trials/percentage columns
4. Daily quick-tally sheet (for goals measured per session)
5. Graph page — hand-plotted, gridded, with target line
6. Multi-goal roster overview (student numbers, not names)
7. "Is this goal on pace?" printable decision table — the zero-technology half
8. **AI Progress Summary page** — the differentiator
9. **Group snapshot** — synthesis across a caseload
10. Meeting-prep one-pager (what the data says, in plain sentences)
11–13. Blank/editable variants of the core sheets
14. How this is built + what we are not claiming

**The AI page, concretely.** The prompt takes six weeks of de-identified
scores and returns a trend read, an on-pace judgment, and a *drafted
progress-report sentence*. That last item is the chore SPED teachers
dread most and nothing in this niche does it. Example shape:
"A 3rd grader's goal is 8/10 on two-digit addition with regrouping.
Weekly scores: 3, 4, 4, 6, 5, 7. Summarise the trend, say whether it is
on pace to meet the goal by June, and draft one progress-report sentence."

**GUARDRAILS — binding, and stated on the product:**
- This is a **tracking tool**. It never advises what a goal should say,
  whether a student qualifies for services, or what placement is
  appropriate. Those are IEP-team decisions.
- De-identified only: student numbers, grade, goal area, scores. Never a
  name, never a full roster, never a diagnosis in a prompt.
- Legal note: "informational; follow your district's IEP procedures and
  your team's guidance."
- Say plainly what it is not: not an IEP writer, not a compliance
  document, not legal advice.
- Audit any example data printed on the page: tally it and confirm the
  stated conclusion actually follows.
