### Why it is not the dashboard's Points tab again

The shipped dashboard holds a **running total** — no dates, no history,
no way to answer "is this better than three weeks ago". This build holds
**dated records**. Stated as the lesson: build 2 taught the app to
remember a *value*, build 3 teaches it to remember a *record over time*,
which means the teacher decides a data shape before they prompt. That is
the first architectural idea in the series and it is what separates
vibe-coding a toy from vibe-coding a tool.

### Grounding that came out of the research, not out of a keyword

**Check-In / Check-Out is the most frequently used Tier 2 behavior
intervention in US schools** — a daily point card, three to five
expectations, six to eight weeks, reviewed on data. That is the artifact's
real shape, and building to it is why teachers will want it. It is *not* a
TPT row and the brief says so; and the pack records and summarizes, it
never prescribes the team's intervention.

### Pricing anchored honestly

**ClassDojo's teacher product is free** — we do not claim to undercut it,
and the brief forbids it. The difference is where the records live.
**LiveSchool licenses at roughly $2–4 per student per year**: $50–100 for
a class, $1,000–2,000 for a 500-student building, annually. That is the
anchor for a $22 one-time build-along.

### Two hazards specific to this build, both in the brief

1. **A shared classroom computer.** Browser storage is per profile, not
   per person — a substitute or a student on the same profile can open
   the tracker. This does not apply to a name picker; it very much
   applies to behavior records.
2. **Clearing browsing data wipes six weeks of intervention.** The trend
   line is the entire value, so the weekly export is a task, not a tip.
