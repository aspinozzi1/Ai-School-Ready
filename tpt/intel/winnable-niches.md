# Lane 5 · Open Demand — high-want teacher items we can actually win
(owner directive 2026-09-01, replacing the Classical Education lane,
which the owner cut the same day as a poor fit)

Lane 5 is not an audience. It is a **method**: every Sunday, take the
highest demand-to-competition ratio phrase we hold that no product
serves yet, and build the thing teachers are literally searching for.
No theme, no persona — just the open door with the most traffic behind
it.

## The Win Score

For every phrase the owner's TPT Keywords pulls return:

    win score = (monthly searches ÷ midpoint of the Resources bucket) × 1000

Bucket midpoints: <1K = 500 · 1K–3K = 2,000 · 3K–8K = 5,500 ·
8K–20K = 14,000 · 20K–40K = 30,000 · 40K–75K = 57,500 ·
75K–150K = 112,500 · 150K–300K = 225,000 · 300K–500K = 400,000 ·
>500K = 600,000.

Read it as "searches per thousand competing products." A score above
~500 means real demand against inventory we can plausibly out-rank as a
new store; above 1,000 is a genuine opening.

**Two hard filters before the score counts:**
1. Opportunity badge must be **Strong** or **Promising**. Moderate,
   Challenging, and Very Difficult are out regardless of score.
2. We must be able to make the *artifact* teachers want, at our quality
   bar, in one build. If the phrase demands editable slides and we only
   ship PDF, it waits for the pipeline, not the calendar.

## The finding that reset this table (2026-09-05, pulls #12–#13)

**`iep at a glance` scores 4,600 — double anything we had ever seen, and
five times the phrase we were about to lead with.** 9,199 searches
against a 1K–3K shelf with a Strong badge. `iep snapshot` (1,276) is the
same artifact under another name. Both appear in two different seeds'
related lists, so this is central to how the family is searched.

The shape is what makes it rare. Every other large number in this table
sits behind 20K+ competing products; this one has real volume and almost
no inventory. That combination is what the win score exists to find, and
it has not turned up before.

**What it is:** a one-page summary of a student's IEP — goals,
accommodations, services, contacts, what to do in a hard moment — that
general education teachers and paraprofessionals keep at hand. A blank
form the case manager fills in. Squarely inside our guardrails: it is a
template, never advice about what a goal or placement should be.

**The catch, and it is real.** A completed at-a-glance sheet holds a
named student's confidential special education data. It is the most
sensitive artifact we have designed, and the AI half cannot work here
the way it does elsewhere. Requirements are in `2026-W37-brief.md`.

## What the ranking tells us to stop doing

- **Head terms are decoration.** "classroom rules" has 18,509 searches
  and a score of 82; "classroom procedures checklist" has 1,598 and a
  score of 799. The small phrase is ~10× the better bet.
- **Never title against a Moderate badge**, however big the number.
  "parent communication" (1,195 searches, score 11) is the cautionary
  tale sitting in our own catalog.
- **Seasonal giants are tags, not titles**: back to school, meet the
  teacher, first day of school activities.

## How Friday's worksheet uses this

The Friday lookup list now hunts for *new* high-ratio phrases in the
teacher-workflow families we can serve — organization, classroom
management, family communication, assessment, conferences, end-of-term,
seasonal routines. Every returned phrase gets scored and slotted into
the table above. The top open row becomes the next Sunday's lane-5
build.
