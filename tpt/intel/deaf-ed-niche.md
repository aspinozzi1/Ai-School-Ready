# Niche analysis — Deaf Education line (owner directive 2026-08-26)

The owner is passionate about deaf education — specifically deaf students
in general education — as the parent of a deaf son. This line serves
Teachers of the Deaf (ToDs), itinerant ToDs, and the general-education
teachers who have DHH (deaf/hard-of-hearing) students in their classes.

## Market reality (researched 2026-08-26)

- The TPT deaf-ed niche is **real, active, and small**: dedicated
  itinerant-ToD sellers exist (in-service handouts for gen-ed teachers,
  itinerant student folders, back-to-school DHH toolkits, self-advocacy
  social stories about IEP/504 supports like remote microphones and
  preferential seating). Demand phrases: "dhh resources", "itinerant
  teacher of the deaf", "deaf and hard of hearing", "dhh in-service",
  "self-advocacy deaf students".
- **Nobody owns "deaf ed x AI."** Same trailblazer opening as our other
  lines: ToDs drown in exactly the work AI drafts well — adapting
  materials, pre-teaching vocabulary lists, writing gen-ed teacher
  briefings, drafting IEP-meeting communication — and no one is teaching
  them to do it safely.
- The itinerant ToD is a **perfect customer**: serves many schools,
  constantly re-explains the same things to new gen-ed teachers, buys
  reusable printables, and is chronically short on prep time.

## Line design laws (in addition to every standing rule)

1. **Accuracy and respect are the brand.** DHH students and families are
   diverse — ASL users, listening-and-spoken-language, bimodal, cochlear
   implants, hearing aids, no devices. Products never assume one path is
   correct, never use outdated terms ("hearing impaired" only when
   quoting legal category names, with a note), and treat Deaf culture
   with respect.
2. **Pedagogy, never audiology.** We produce teaching supports: visual
   supports, captioning habits, pre-teaching vocabulary, communication
   repair strategies, seating and acoustics awareness, self-advocacy
   scaffolds, gen-ed teacher briefings. We never give medical, device,
   or audiological advice — that line appears in the legal note of every
   deaf-ed product ("informational; follow your audiologist's and IEP
   team's guidance").
3. **Student privacy doubled.** DHH students are a small, identifiable
   population — the describe-the-learner-never-the-child rule matters
   even more, and every AI-prompt product in this line repeats it.
4. **The owner's family stays private.** The line exists because of
   lived experience, but no listing references the owner's son or any
   identifiable family details. If the owner ever wants a provenance
   line like "built by the parents of a deaf child," that is their
   explicit call to make, in their words — never assumed, and never
   with the child's name or details.
5. **AI rides along, doesn't lead.** Some products are pure deaf-ed
   resources (the in-service handout, self-advocacy scaffolds); AI
   prompt packs are one strand, not the whole line. Every AI use in
   this line follows the standing safety rules.
