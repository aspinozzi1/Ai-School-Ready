**Tell me one of builds 1 to 3 has sold and I will build build 4 on Friday
without further argument.** That single fact changes the recommendation

### If you do want build 4, these two are the candidates

- **"Ship It" — the day the app goes to real users.** The natural end of the
  arc: build 1 designs, build 2 runs a business, build 3 tests, build 4 releases
  it to another class and handles what comes back. One sentence: *software is
  not finished when it works, it is finished when somebody else can use it
  without you.*

  decisions — what the app records, what it refuses to record, and why. **Nobody

  reasoning. **Demand almost certainly nil as a search phrase**, which is the

### The thing I found today that makes rows 3 and 4 more interesting

Genius Hour has a published six-step framework — **passion, plan, pitch,
project, product, presentation.** Our three shipped units sit inside it: build 1
is pitch and project, build 2 is product, build 3 is the testing step that the
framework does not have. **If `genius hour` measures anything at all, the lane
has a ready-made structure to be renamed into**, with an honest claim that we
cover the step the framework skips.

**The caveat, because the evidence is weak.** What I could find on whether
teachers actually run these is small, mostly non-US and not adoption data. One
2025 survey of elementary educators found most teachers do not use project-based
learning regularly while many still value it — which would make these aspirational
phrases rather than buying ones. **Treat all of that as a hint, not a number.
Your TPT rows outrank every word of it.**
