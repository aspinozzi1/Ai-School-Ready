# Lane 5 · Classical Education (owner directive 2026-09-01)

AI integration for classical educators: classical Christian schools,
classical charter and university-model schools, Classical Conversations
and other homeschool communities, Charlotte Mason households, and
Great Books teachers.

## The thing to understand before writing a single page

**Classical education is currently positioning itself as the antidote to
AI, not a market for it.** In 2026 the classical press is running pieces
titled "How classical education can be an antidote to AI" and "Classical
education can help students endure the age of AI." Meanwhile teacher
sentiment broadly has moved against classroom AI — a spring 2026 survey
put **55% of teachers opposed**, an 8-point drop in support across one
school year, with 76% reporting no training.

A product that walks into that room offering to put AI in front of
students will be rejected on sight, and rightly. It would also be off
-brand: our whole posture is AI as the *teacher's* assistant, human
judgment intact.

**So the wedge is the opposite of what it looks like.** Classical
teachers carry an unusually heavy prep load — Latin drills, recitation
and memory-work schedules, Socratic question banks, narration prompts,
copywork selection, primary-source glossing, Great Books discussion
plans. That prep is exactly where AI helps and nothing sacred is at
stake. The student's encounter with the text stays untouched.

## Binding design laws for lane 5

1. **AI never touches the student's encounter with the text.** No
   AI-generated summaries of the Great Books, no chatbot Socratic
   partner, no machine-graded recitation. If a product would let a
   student skip reading, thinking, or speaking, we do not make it.
2. **Teacher-side prep only.** Everything is drafted by the adult,
   before class, and reviewed by the adult. The AI produces raw
   material a teacher edits — never something handed straight to a
   child.
3. **Speak the tradition's language accurately or not at all.** Trivium
   (grammar, logic, rhetoric stages), quadrivium, narration, copywork,
   dictation, memory work, recitation, Socratic discussion, Latin
   declensions and conjugations, Great Books. Getting this vocabulary
   subtly wrong is the fastest way to lose this audience — they will
   notice.
4. **Respect the skepticism; never argue with it.** Products
   acknowledge that a teacher may reasonably choose to use none of
   this. No hype, no "the future is here," no implication that
   AI-free classrooms are behind. The tone is: here is your prep time
   back, spend it on your students.
5. **Denominational neutrality.** Classical Christian schools are a
   large share of this market, but classical charter and secular
   classical schools are real too. Content stays neutral: no doctrinal
   claims, no assumed catechism, no scripture selections. Where a
   product touches virtue or character, keep it in the classical
   (Aristotelian/cardinal-virtues) register that all camps share.

## First-wave product backlog

Frees:
- **Socratic question starter set** — 40 discussion questions by
  trivium stage, plus one AI prompt a teacher uses to generate more for
  any text they're teaching
- **Memory work planner** — a year of recitation scheduling on one
  page, with a prompt for building the review rotation
- **Narration prompt cards** (Charlotte Mason register) — oral and
  written narration starters by age band
- **Latin drill generator guide** — one page: how to get a decent
  conjugation/declension drill sheet out of an AI tool, and how to
  proof it (Latin is where AI most often produces confident nonsense —
  the proofing step is the product)
- **"AI in a classical school" one-pager for parents** — the honest
  version: what we use it for (teacher prep), what we will never use it
  for (student thinking)

Paid ($8–12):
- **The Classical Teacher's Prep Kit** — Socratic question banks,
  narration and copywork selection, memory-work scheduling, and the
  proofing checklists, with master prompts for each
- **Great Books Discussion Planner** — per-text planning pages plus
  prompts that draft background context and question ladders for the
  *teacher* (never student-facing summaries)
- **Latin & Recitation Drill Builder** — drill templates plus the
  proofing protocol, aimed at the single biggest prep sink in a
  classical week

## Meter note — validation still owed

themselves (classicalacademicpress.com, classicalconversations.com,
welltrainedmind.com, ed.gov) rather than resource sellers, and

community forums. That tells us the audience gathers in named
communities, not in generic search — a pattern that usually means
**TPT's own numbers will differ sharply from Google's**, exactly as
they did for Deaf Education.
