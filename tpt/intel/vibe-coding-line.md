## LANE A · Teacher Vibe Coding

Teaching teachers to build the software that runs their room.

### What the research says teachers actually build

From 2026 coverage of teacher-built tools: **hall passes, seating charts,
group makers, behavior trackers, random name pickers** — "the small tools
that keep a room running." A commercial platform (Bool for Teachers) now
sells exactly this, describing what you need and getting a live URL in under
a minute.

That list is the series roadmap. It is not invented; it is what teachers are
already reaching for.

### The thing that makes it software instead of a worksheet

**It has to remember.** A seating chart that forgets the seats is a picture.
A points tracker that resets on refresh is a toy. The step that turns a page
into an application is **saving state on the teacher's own device** — and
that is also what keeps it safe: nothing is transmitted, no account exists,
no server holds anything.

**This is the single most important teaching point in the whole line**, and
it is what separates our products from a blog post that produces a pretty
static page.

## LANE B · Young Entrepreneurs, rebuilt around vibe coding

The old lane measured badly and the numbers say why: `market day` **6**,
`classroom economy` **73**, `entrepreneurship` **210**. Those are crowded
theme words on a tired format.

**The new lane is different in kind, not degree: students design software,
the teacher builds it.** A business unit where the deliverable is a working
app rather than a poster.

### The design law that makes this safe and better pedagogy

**The grown-up holds the keyboard.** Chatbots are 13+, and our standing rule
has always been that a child's identity never enters a prompt. So:

- **Students do:** the market research, the problem statement, the feature
  list, the user testing, the pitch, the iteration decisions.
- **The teacher does:** the prompting and the building, projected on the
  board so the class sees their spec become software.

This is not a compromise. **It is the better lesson** — students spend their
time on specification, testing and judgment, which is the actual thinking,
instead of on typing prompts.

| 2 | **The class business app** | Market day, but the storefront is software the class specced |
| 3 | **Test it like a user** | Usability testing, feedback rounds, iteration — the part schools never teach |
