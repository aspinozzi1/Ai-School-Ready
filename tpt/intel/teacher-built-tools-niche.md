# Lane 6 · Teacher-Built Classroom Tools
**The AI-Ready School flagship line.** Owner directive 2026-09-11/12.
All eight decisions below are binding.

Teaching teachers to build their own classroom software with AI, instead
of waiting for a district to buy it.

---

## The thesis, and what the research actually says

The owner's thesis: teachers building their own in-class platforms is
the future of education, and nobody on TPT is teaching it.

**Researched 2026-09-11. The thesis holds, with one correction.**

**Confirmed — this is a live, named movement:**
- "Vibe coding for teachers" is an established term with 2026 coverage
  from Cool Cat Teacher, Class Tech Tips, Brittany Washburn and TCEA.
- AI for Education sells a *Vibe-Coding 101 for Educators* workshop;
  Teacher Academy (Erasmus) sells a course; Coursera hosts *The
  Non-Coder's Guide to Vibe Coding*; CodeHS released a vibe-coding tool
  and starter course on 9 July 2026.
- **The strongest single data point for the owner's thesis:** Peninsula
  School District (Washington) used Claude Code to build its own
  classroom and operations tools, including a lesson-feedback tool
  called LessonLens. Their CIO estimates roughly **$220,000 a year**
  saved by replacing commercial subscriptions with tools built in-house
  in hours. That is the thesis already happening at district scale.
- An ISTE survey of 2,500 K–12 computer science teachers found **63% of
  classrooms had integrated AI coding tools** by the end of 2024–25, up
  from 8% in 2022–23.

**Unserved on TPT.** The PD is being sold as workshops and university
courses. Web search surfaced no TPT products teaching this. Absence of
evidence is not proof — **the owner's TPT Keywords pulls are the real
meter and none of this is validated yet** (see the keyword list below).

**The correction — do not frame this as replacing Google Classroom or
Teams.** Those hold rosters, grades and records: FERPA-covered education
records, and FERPA now reaches AI-generated data that identifies a
student. A teacher standing up a personal platform holding that data,
outside district review, creates genuine exposure for themselves and
their district. The sellable, honest framing is **teachers building the
tools their LMS does not provide.** The LMS stays the system of record.

## The eight binding decisions (owner, 2026-09-11/12)

1. **Scope:** tools alongside the LMS, not a replacement for it.
2. **Format:** build-along walkthroughs. Each product walks a teacher
   through building ONE specific thing end to end — the exact prompts,
   the working result, and what to do when it breaks.
3. **Tool:** name one tool for concrete steps, with a short "if you use
   a different one" note. Claude leads, since the research repeatedly
   names it as strongest for this.
4. **Audience:** teachers already comfortable with AI who want to go
   further. Not absolute beginners.
5. **First build:** a **self-checking practice activity** — runs from
   one file, no login, any browser, any topic.
6. **Price:** premium flagship, **$18–25**. Licences at half.
7. **Cadence:** Sunday's **headline** product, with one validated
   win-lane item alongside so the IEP and behavior families keep growing.
8. **Guardrail:** teach it, with the limits stated plainly.

## THE DATA GUARDRAIL — the owner's own words, and the thing that makes
## this line trustworthy

> "Teach it, understanding that student data will eventually need to be
> used but the content teaches how to use student data appropriately and
> not use truly PII."

This is the correct call and it is better than a blanket "never touch
student data," which would have been advice no working teacher could
follow. Any tool worth building eventually meets real student work. The
product's job is to teach **the discipline**, not to pretend the problem
away.

**Every product in this line teaches the same three tiers:**

| Tier | What goes in | Who approves |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Teacher-authored content** | Word lists, questions, prompts, examples the teacher wrote. No student data of any kind. | Nobody. Build it today. |
| **2 · De-identified student data** | Scores, error patterns, response counts — labelled S1/S2/S3, or "a 3rd grader," never a name. | Nobody, *if* the identifiers never enter. This is where our AI Growth Eval already lives. |
| **3 · Identifiable student data** | Names, rosters, IEP details, grades tied to a person, anything a stranger could trace to one child. | **Your district. Stop and ask.** This is not a thing to build on a personal account. |

**The teaching points, in every build-along:**
- How to recognise you have crossed from Tier 2 into Tier 3 — usually by
  adding "just the names so I can tell them apart."
- The substitution habit: a roster key stays on paper or in the
  district's own system; the tool sees S1, S2, S3.
- Where the file lives matters as much as what is in it. A tool that
  runs locally in a browser and saves nothing is a different risk from
  one hosted publicly with a database behind it.
- What a district actually needs to approve, and why that is a
  reasonable thing for them to ask rather than an obstacle.
- Plainly: **this is practical guidance, not legal advice.** FERPA,
  COPPA, state law and district policy are the authorities; point to
  them and tell the teacher to check their own district's policy.

## The product ladder

**Flagship build-alongs — $18–25, ~30–45 pages each.** One artifact
each, start to finish:

1. **Self-checking practice activity** *(first build — decided)*
   Any topic. Student clicks, gets told right or wrong, tries again.
   One file, no login, no data leaves the machine. Pure Tier 1.
2. **Class hub landing page** — agenda, links, due dates, office hours.
3. **De-identified data dashboard** — takes scores the teacher already
   collected, shows patterns and next moves. Tier 2, and the direct
   descendant of the AI Growth Eval already in the catalog.
4. **Parent-facing page** — what we are learning, how to help at home.
5. **Generator** — the teacher's own worksheets, varied endlessly.

**Frees that funnel in:** one short "make your first thing in twenty
minutes" build, and the three-tier data-safety page as a standalone.

**Bundle** once three flagships exist, ~25% below the sum.

## What each build-along must contain

- **The finished thing first.** Show what they will have at the end,
  before any instruction.
- **The exact prompts, in full, in a copyable block.** Not paraphrased.
  This is the part buyers cannot get from a blog post that says "just
  ask the AI."
- **The actual working file**, so a teacher who gets stuck still owns a
  usable tool. This is what makes it a TPT product rather than a lesson.
- **What to do when it breaks** — the three or four failures that
  actually happen, and the sentence that fixes each.
- **How to change it** for another subject, grade or topic.
- **The three-tier data page**, scaled to the build.
- **"How this is built"** — sources, and what we are not claiming.

## The `vibe coding` decision (owner, 2026-09-12)

**Measured 2026-09-11: `vibe coding` returns "not enough data for this term" on
TPT Keywords.** No search volume today. `ai tools for teachers` returned the
same, though at four words that one may be hitting the tool's length limit
rather than a true zero; `vibe coding` is two words, so its zero is real.

**The owner's judgment is that the term becomes mainstream, and the line should
claim it now.** That is very likely right on the trend, and the research
supports it: Cool Cat Teacher, Class Tech Tips, TCEA, CodeHS, Coursera and
AI for Education all use the phrase in 2026.

**So this is not an either/or, and it was resolved by splitting the surfaces:**

| Surface | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Tags** | **`vibe coding` in, from day one** | A tag costs one of six slots and nothing else. It starts accumulating listing age against the phrase now, which is the whole advantage of being early. Replaced `ai tools for teachers`, which measured empty. |
| **Description** | **Opens with the term** | TPT indexes description text. The phrase can grow here without spending title characters. |
| **Inside the product** | **Names and teaches the term** | Brand building. A buyer who learns the word from us associates it with us. |
| **Title** | **Artifact wording for now** | The title is the one surface that has to convert a browsing teacher *today*, and today most of them do not know the word. |

**The flip condition, so this is a plan and not a hope:**

> Re-pull `vibe coding` monthly. When it returns **real volume with a Strong or
> Promising badge**, move it into the title of every product in this line and
> retitle the back catalog. Editing a TPT title costs nothing and does not reset
> the listing.

That asymmetry is the whole argument: we can add the word to a title the day it
starts selling, but we cannot retroactively have been tagged for it since
September. So claim it in the cheap places now, spend title characters only when
the data says to.

**A risk worth naming.** In professional software circles "vibe coding" carries
a connotation of accepting AI output without understanding it. For a product
teaching teachers, that association could cut against us. The answer is to own
the responsible version explicitly, which the pack now does: the method page
names both uses of the term and states that this one means describe, check,
correct, repeat. That turns a brand risk into a differentiator.
