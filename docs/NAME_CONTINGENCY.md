# Name contingency file — "AI-Ready School" vs. the world

Purpose: everything about the name-collision question in one place — the
evidence, the watch protocol, and the exact playbook if a challenge ever
arrives. Written 2026-08-12 so nobody has to reconstruct this under stress.
Layperson's file, not legal advice; it exists to make the eventual attorney
conversation short and cheap.

## The situation, in three sentences

An India-based company operates as "AI Ready School" at aireadyschool.com
(software/hardware platform for Indian K-12 schools). We operate as
AI-Ready School LLC (NJ) at ai-readyschool.com, selling teacher PD content
in the US. Trademark rights are territorial, and all evidence to date says
the US name is unclaimed — our plan is to be first in US commerce and file
our own federal trademark as soon as revenue allows.

## The bootstrap ladder (protection funded by revenue, in order)

1. FIRST sale  → bind E&O + general liability (~$50-90/mo, monthly billing).
2. SECOND sale → file our own federal trademark, USPTO TEAS, ~$350,
   class 041 (education/training services), DIY is acceptable. From filing
   day forward, a future US entrant under this name has OUR mark to deal
   with. This single act inverts the entire risk.
3. THIRD sale  → attorney hour: confirm the TM filing, run the formal
   knockout search, review the rest of LEGAL_AUDIT.md.

## IF A CHALLENGE EVER ARRIVES (the "in case" playbook)

A challenge means: a cease-and-desist letter, a USPTO opposition, or a
lawyer's email claiming rights to the name. If that day comes:

1. DO NOT respond same-day. Nothing bad happens from a week of silence.
   Do not admit, agree, sign, or promise anything in a reply.
2. Preserve everything: the letter, its envelope/headers, the date.
3. Read this file. Then hire a trademark attorney for a 1-hour review —
   by that point revenue exists; if it somehow doesn't, NJ Volunteer
   Lawyers for the Arts and SCORE offer low/no-cost small-business IP
   consults.
4. Facts that favor us, assembled for that attorney: documented first use
   in US commerce (see evidence folder), good-faith research predating
   launch (this file's dated entries), different goods (PD content vs
   software platform), different territory at the time of our adoption,
   and the descriptiveness of "AI-ready" as a phrase.
5. Realistic outcomes, in order of likelihood: the letter overreaches and
   coexistence is negotiated; OR a phased rebrand is negotiated (months,
   not days, is standard). Damages against a good-faith small user with
   priority evidence are not a realistic outcome.
6. If a rebrand is ever the answer, the mechanics are cheap by design:
   every product regenerates from source (kits/, site config, brand
   tokens). Inventory of name-bearing surfaces: config/site.ts, logo
   components, brand.css, kit HTML sources + deck.js files (rebuilt by
   tooling), TpT listings, LLC name (NJ amendment ~$100 — do NOT use a
   DBA-over-old-LLC-name structure; school POs must match the brand),
   domain, Resend/email, Stripe/Netlify/Supabase display names.
   Estimated true cost at small scale: ~$250 cash + days of rebuilds.

## Why we are not rebranding preemptively

Every verified fact (no US filing, no US commerce, different product,
different market) says the US name is ours to take by using it first.
Rebranding now would surrender a position we currently hold by default,
cost real momentum, and buy protection against a scenario the $350 filing
eliminates anyway. Decision recorded 2026-08-12; the only tripwire that
reopens it is a verified LIVE US filing by the Indian company or another
education-sector claimant (see watch protocol).
