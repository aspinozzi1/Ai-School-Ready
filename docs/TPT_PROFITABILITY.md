# TpT profitability playbook — AI-Ready School

Companion to `docs/TPT_LAUNCH.md`. The launch file says what to ship and when. This
file says where the money actually comes from.

The starting economics are unusually good: the marginal cost of another sale is zero.
These are PDFs and a deck. Every lever below is a revenue lever, and the only real cost
is the owner's time, so the whole game is raising revenue per hour spent.

## Lever 1: sell to schools, not to teachers. This is the big one.

Most TpT sellers make $3-8 products for one teacher's classroom. We make a
**whole-staff PD session**. One teacher buying one license is the wrong unit of sale,
and pricing as though it were leaves most of the money on the table.

Three facts make this work:

1. **Additional licenses exist, and the seller sets their price.** Each extra copy of
   the same resource is an additional license, defaulted to 10% off, and a
   Teacher-Author can set that to any price or discount they choose.
2. **Schools can pay by purchase order through TpT.** Minimum PO is $20 with a $2.99
   fee. TpT handles the invoicing, which is the exact machinery we were about to build
   ourselves with Stripe Invoicing and then cancelled.
3. **TpT for Schools lets a buyer purchase many licenses at once**, and a central
   account keeps the files accessible to the school.

**The move:** set additional-license pricing aggressively low on the kits, far below the
10% default. A staff PD kit is worthless at one license and valuable at twenty.

| Scenario | Revenue at 80% royalty |
|---|---|
| One teacher, one license, $24 | $19.20 |
| A school, 20 licenses, 10% default ($21.60 each) | ~$347 |
| A school, 20 licenses, additional at $8 | ~$140 |
| A school, 20 licenses, additional at $12 | ~$201 |

Note what that table actually says: the **default 10% is the most profitable per sale**,
but it is also the least likely to close, because a principal doing the math on 20
licenses at $21.60 sees $432 and balks. The question is not which number is biggest, it
is which number a school actually buys. A $140-200 school sale that happens beats a $347
sale that does not. **Start additional licenses around $10-12 and watch what converts.**

**Say it in the listing.** School buyers search for what they can actually purchase. Put
"purchase orders accepted through TpT for Schools" and "discounted multi-teacher
licensing available" in the description of every paid kit. Most sellers never mention it
because their product is for one classroom. Ours is not.

## Lever 2: the bundle is the flagship, not the kits

Bundles raise the average order and are the natural school purchase. Track A Complete
(all eight kits) is the product a principal buys with building funds, and it should be
priced and presented as the main event, with individual kits as the sample.

At $99-129 for eight kits against roughly $192 apart, the discount is real and the
absolute number still dwarfs a single-kit sale. One bundle sale at 80% is about $79-103,
the equivalent of four to five individual kit sales, from one buyer, in one transaction.

## Lever 3: Premium membership, immediately

$59.95 a year versus 55% royalty and a $0.30 per-resource fee. On a $24 kit that is
$19.20 versus $12.90. Premium pays for itself in about ten kit sales, and one school
multi-license order clears it in a single afternoon. The $29 Basic fee is non-refundable
and does not credit toward Premium, so going Basic first is money burned.

## Lever 4: free products are the acquisition budget

There is no ad spend here, so free products are how the store buys attention. Each one
earns followers, and followers get notified every time something new goes up. Six free
products at launch is not generosity; it is the customer-acquisition engine running at
zero cost.

Every free product carries the same back-page pointer to the paid kits. The De-ID Drills
packet already does this well and is the template.

## Lever 5: reviews compound, and they are askable

Reviews drive search ranking, and TpT gives buyers credit toward future purchases for
leaving them, so asking is doing the buyer a favor rather than begging. Put the ask
inside the product, on the last page, in one line. A store with fifty reviews outranks a
store with five, permanently, and reviews on free products count.

## Lever 6: updates put you back in front of past buyers

Updating a file notifies everyone who already bought it. Those are the warmest buyers
that exist: they already paid once and liked it enough not to refund. The paused deck
redesign is therefore not a delay, it is a scheduled re-contact with the entire customer
base, one kit at a time.

## Lever 7: sell into the calendar

K-12 buying is violently seasonal. The peaks are **back-to-school (July into September)**
and **January**. Plan the flagship bundle and the biggest launches into those windows,
and use the slow months to build inventory rather than to launch it. Participate in TpT's
sitewide sale events; discount-driven volume on a zero-marginal-cost product is free money.

## Lever 8: the first two lines of the description do the selling

TpT truncates. The searchable title and the opening two lines carry the whole pitch.
Every paid kit leads with the same two claims, because they are the two a buyer cares
about: **you can run this yourself this week, and it was built by two certified
teachers.** The AI disclosure stays where it belongs, on the "how this was made" page,
never in the lede.

## What NOT to spend time on

- **Chasing the $3-8 product market.** Our production standard makes those unprofitable
  per hour. Micro-products exist to feed the funnel, not to be the business, and every
  paid one must clear the Swipe Test: tangible classroom documentation, not training
  material.
- **Rebuilding what TpT already does.** Invoicing, license management, file delivery,
  and PO handling are the platform's job now. That is the whole point of this pivot.
- **Perfecting a kit that has not sold once.** Ship, then improve on the update cycle,
  which doubles as re-contact.

## The honest arithmetic

At $24 a kit and 80% royalty, one kit sale is $19.20. Ten individual sales a month is
$192. **One school buying twenty licenses of one kit is worth more than a whole month of
individual sales**, and a school that buys one kit is the natural buyer of the bundle
next year. Everything in this playbook points at the same conclusion: the individual
teacher is the top of the funnel, and the school is the business.
